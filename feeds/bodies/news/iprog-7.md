Chinese robot manufacturer UBTECH has officially commissioned its Industrial Humanoid Robot Super Smart Factory in Liuzhou, Guangxi. Covering 14,000 square meters, the plant is designed to operate at an unprecedented scale, rolling off a completed industrial humanoid once every 10 minutes, a rate made possible by a highly automated process. VIDEO Last December UBTECH marked the milestone of having built 1,000 humanoid robots. Its new facility has a capacity of 10,000 units per annum. And, according to the video _This milestone is just the beginning. Large-scale industrialization of humanoid robots is right around the corner._ While the previous UBTECH video, see [Robot Army Video As Robots Shipped En Masse](<https://www.i-programmer.info/news/169-robotics/18462-ubtech-video.html>), may have been intended to shock and awe the audience, it was scaled to the point of unbelievability. This video is much more convincing and it seems to be no exaggeration that the facility will be able to produce its robots at the rate of one every 10 minutes. Contrast this with US-based Figure AI's rate of one robot per hour. UBTECH's production line is highly automated and employs its own robots. Assembling a single humanoid chassis requires more than 2,000 screws with 50 distinct specifications. This relies on digital torque stations. Instead of manual parts runners, UBTECH has deployed an embodied mobile logistics fleet. Wheeled Cruzr Y1 and Cruzr S2 mobile manipulators outfitted with 3D vision handle depalletizing, bin picking, tote replenishment, and intra-station component transit. These mobile manipulators dynamically adapt to shifted storage bins and non-uniform pallet orientations, working alongside standard automated guided vehicles (AGVs),autonomous forklifts, and unmanned haulers. Does this mass production mean the robot revolution is properly underway? Up until now the cost of robots has restricted their adoption, allowing humans to be complacent about the prospect of being overrun by humanoids. UBTECH is banking on scale to reduce humanoid manufacturing costs by 20% to 30% annually, giving a sub-$20,000 unit production cost by 2030. Together with the advances in AI, making robots more and more versatile and efficient does alter the balance. But having a robot workforce that can do the heavy, dirty, dangerous or repetitive jobs that we don't want to do, still seems to me to be an attractive prospect.
