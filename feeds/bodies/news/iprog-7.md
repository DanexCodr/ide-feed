A Kaggle competition to uncover non-obvious linkages between combine sensor tracking and regular-season NFL game performance is now open, with top prizes of $10,000.  NFL combine sensor tracking uses Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) chips placed in players' shoulder pads to record real-time movement data every tenth of a second during drills. The system measures continuous movement, speed, acceleration, deceleration, and distance every 0.1 seconds. The data is used as the basis for the Combine IQ dashboard, which assigns players a draft score and athleticism grade from 50 to 99. While the main NFL combine sensor tracking is carried out on players who are already in the NFL, the same technique is now being applied to the NFL Scouting Combine. Every spring, college footballers hoping for a professional career gather in Indianapolis for the NFL Scouting Combine. In the past, players were evaluated using standardized stopwatch timings and physical testing numbers, such as the 40-yard dash, short shuttle, 3-cone drill, and vertical jump. They now wear player tracking sensors that are equivalent to those worn in the NFL.  The 2027 Big Data Bowl is about finding interesting insights from that data. The competition organizers provide player tracking data from the NFL Scouting Combine alongside regular-season NFL game tracking data. The goal of this year's competition is to better understand how player movement during combine drills translates to performance on the field during the NFL regular season. Participants in the competition are asked to analyze the movements of entrants in the scouting combine in a way that is actionable and accessible to coaches, scouts, and front offices. The organizers suggest this might include creating a new metric or comparing movement traits across position groups. As is often the case in Big Data Bowl analytics competitions, participants are encouraged to focus on one small aspect of movement, a specific position group, or an individual drill, rather than attempting to evaluate every prospect across all drills. The competition rules give some examples of potential analyses, including 

  * Measuring change of direction, deceleration into breaks, and acceleration out of cuts for receivers and defensive backs compared to in-game separation
  * Evaluating first-step quickness, lateral agility, and burst in trench play for offensive and defensive linemen during agility drills compared to pass-rush or run-blocking performance
  * Comparing sensor-based movement metrics with traditional drill times to identify where tracking data adds context to prospect evaluation
  * Analyzing body control, acceleration profiles, or pursuit angles during position drills and their relationship to NFL rookie performance

The competition is open now, and final submissions are due by January 6, 2027. Results will be announced at the end of January. There are two tracks, a university track that is only to groups or individuals composed entirely of undergraduate students, and an open track that anyone can enter. Each track has three finalist prizes of $10,000 and three runner up prizes of $5,000. There's also a prize for the winning entry of $10,000.  More details from the competition website.
