Today LangGrant, formerly known as Windocks, is launching the Enterprise Reasoning Initiative, a fully open source project to create a safety mechanism for Enterprise AI. The Enterprise Reasoning Initiative aims to develop an open interoperability standard enabling vendors, enterprises, and developers to make human expertise a persistent part of how enterprise AI reasons. As such, it addresses one of the most critical and overlooked problems currently faced by Enterprise AI: the transition from ephemeral, isolated chatbot interactions to enduring, structured organizational intelligence. The initiative is led by two industry veterans who have successfully established popular enterprise standards in the past: Bob Kruger, former head of Systems Management products at Microsoft, where he led the team that created the Windows Management Instrumentation and Common Information Model standards; and Ramesh Parameswaran, now CEO of LangGrant who previously led Microsoft interoperability initiatives. Explaining the need for an Enterprise Reasoning Open Standard, Parameswaran stated: _“The future of Enterprise AI should make human expertise more valuable. When an expert improves AI reasoning, that expertise should become part of the organization’s intelligence, and not disappear when the conversation ends."_ In this respect, reasoning needs to be considered a first-class enterprise artifact - much like source code, documents, or datasets - rather than disposable AI output. Parameswaran continued: _"As AI models become more capable and autonomous, enterprises face a choice. They can increasingly hand analysis and decision-making to AI, with people primarily reviewing the results. Or they can build systems in which human expertise and AI reasoning continuously build on each other. The Enterprise Reasoning Initiative is designed for the second path.”_ Open Standards are already in place to cover other aspects of AI integration. We have seen how the Model Context Protocol (MCP) standardizes tool access and Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocols standardize communication. Enterprise Reasoning is envisaged as an open standard ensures that reasoning remains a shared capability rather than a proprietary platform feature. The idea is to standardize the representation of reasoning so people, software tools, and AI systems can exchange, validate, explain, and build upon it. The proposed standard is initially focused on six capabilities: structured reasoning; human judgment throughout the reasoning process; reasoning lifecycle management; reasoning across multiple enterprise information sources; progressively evolving semantic intelligence; and attribution of reasoning and decisions to business outcomes. It sets out to address four key AI issues organizations face today:

  * Safety — As AI agents become more autonomous, human judgment, enterprise policies, and approvals can be applied to the reasoning behind consequential decisions and actions—not just reviewed after the fact.
  * Transparency - The initiative would eliminate the “black box” most frontier models use by default for problem-solving and prevent AI systems from reinventing new reasoning every time they complete a task.
  * Availability - This open source standard would be available for implementation by anyone.
  * Reusability - The project aims to create a standard to make the reasoning they apply in their AI implementations a durable object that can be reused and managed over time.

LangGrant is launching the Enterprise Reasoning Initiative with support from multiple AI innovators. Initial supporters include: Causal Dynamic Labs; Conflux; connectiveup.com; DeepGraph;  
GirardAI; HMX.ai; Knowledge3.ai; LEIT Data; Ngentix; Proof Analytics and Skyhook.  The initiative is seeking additional participation from enterprise software vendors, AI companies, enterprise architects, executives with deep industry and domain expertise, researchers, and standards organizations. A detailed white paper outlining the Initiative’s goals, along with an initial standards work plan, is available at [EnterpriseReasoning.org](<https://enterprisereasoning.org/>).
