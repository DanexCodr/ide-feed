Most firewalls only watch what comes *in*. PacketHound sniffs out what goes *out*: the first time an app tries to connect to the internet, it asks you to **Allow** or **Block**, and remembers your answer.

PacketHound turns Windows Firewall into an "ask me first" firewall for outgoing connections, without drowning you in questions about Windows itself.

### Asks once, remembers

The first time an app tries to connect, you see who made it, where it's going and on which port. Click Allow or Block, for the whole app or just one address, host or domain, and keep it forever or only until the app quits.

Windows' own components are allowed automatically, so you're only asked about the apps you installed, not about hundreds of system services.

A program only counts as Windows if it lives in a Windows folder, is owned by the system and carries a valid Microsoft Windows signature. Copying malware into System32 doesn't get it a free pass.

Malware loves PowerShell, curl, certutil, mshta and rundll32 for downloading payloads. PacketHound still asks before those connect, even though they're part of Windows.

No kernel driver, nothing hidden. Every decision is an ordinary Windows Firewall rule you can see in wf.msc, and it keeps working even when PacketHound is closed.

Prompts appear on top without taking keyboard focus, so you can't answer one by accident while you're typing.

Flip any app between Allow and Block with one switch. Search, sort, see when each decision was made, and clear out rules for programs you've uninstalled.

The activity log shows every blocked attempt with its hostname, port and what happened next. Not sure yet? Decide later, and come back to it from the Rules tab.

## How it works

No drivers, no background service of its own: PacketHound builds on the firewall that's already part of Windows.

1. Unknown apps are blocked PacketHound switches Windows Firewall's outgoing default to *block*, so a program without a rule can't reach the internet.
2. PacketHound notices Windows logs each blocked attempt. PacketHound watches that log, checks whether the program is part of Windows, and allows it quietly if so.
3. You decide For everything else you get a prompt showing the app, its publisher and where it's connecting. Your answer becomes a firewall rule, for the whole app or one destination, kept forever or until the app quits.
