If you ask some prominent US politicians who won the 2020 presidential election, you [might get](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/trumps-intelligence-nominee-refuses-to-say-who-won-2020-election) some [weasely answers](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/survey-who-won-election-republicans-congress/2020/12/04/1a1011f6-3650-11eb-8d38-6aea1adb3839_story.html) that [fail to directly acknowledge](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/01/30/us/politics/patel-2020-election.html?eafs_enabled=false) Joe Biden’s win that November. If you ask Google’s AI Mode the same question, you might get an evasive answer that simply tells you to consult with outside sources.

When I put the direct question of “Who won the 2020 presidential election?” to Google’s AI Mode this morning (after [seeing complaints about cagey answers on social media](https://bsky.app/profile/numb.comfortab.ly/post/3myvsxtev6s27)), I got the following surprisingly indirect response:

> “To review the official results and statistics of the 2020 United States presidential election, including the final electoral college breakdown, popular vote counts, and winner certification, please consult the official report compiled by the Federal Election Commission (FEC).”

My testing resulted in a similarly indirect answer when asking about the 2024 presidential election, with Google’s AI telling me to just consult the FEC for that information. Asking about 2012 election resulted in a link to [The American Presidency Project’s 2012 Data Center](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/statistics/elections/2012) rather than a direct answer. But AI Mode seemed perfectly willing to give me a detailed and correct responses for the winner of every other presidential election going back to 2000.

Other Ars staffers found that AI Mode would similarly provide direct answers to some presidential result questions but not others. The specific election cycles that received each type of response seemed to differ by individual user, though; one staffer reported only getting immediate answers from AI Mode when asking about the 2016 and 2024 presidential elections, with others going back to 2000 simply providing links to outside data.
