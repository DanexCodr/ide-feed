Motivated by the recent breakthrough on the VP vs VNP problem, the users (Samuel Everett and Deven Mithal) guided instances of ChatGPT and Claude to develop further, more granular algebraic complexity class separations. This is a work in progress in both the development and dissemination of mathematical research with AI tools, and any feedback is appreciated.
The results in this repository grew out of a conversation between Sam and Claude Fable 5.2. The initial prompts were inspired by a separate discussion between Sam and Deven, the latter of whom suggested combining the latest VP-VNP results with some of Sam's private work in the area. Claude Fable 5.2 found the proofs through the conversation with Sam, and then wrote most of the paper under his guidance.
That conversation started from initial ideas in a separate, earlier conversation Sam had with ChatGPT Astra 6.2, which proved $\mathrm{BIP}*{\mathbb R}^{\times}\subsetneq\mathrm{PAR}*{\mathbb R}^{\times}$ (Boolean-message interactive proofs against parallel polynomial time, in the real model with multiplication). The conversation with Claude Fable 5.2 extended that starting point to the further separations listed below.
All results are formalized in Lean 4 (see [Lean formalization](https://github.com/samueleverett01/bss-permanent-separations#lean-formalization) below), with the results cited from the literature as explicit hypotheses. The results have not been peer reviewed and should be read with that in mind.

## Results

In the Blum–Shub–Smale (BSS) model, the graph `{Y = per_m(X)}` of the real permanent lies in parallel polynomial time PAR_ℝ and outside each of

- NP_ℝ and coNP_ℝ,
- the second existential level Σ₂ of the real polynomial hierarchy,
- constant-round Arthur–Merlin proofs with real messages,
- interactive proofs with Boolean messages (BIP_ℝ),

and over the complex numbers outside NP_ℂ and coNP_ℂ. Each of these classes is therefore strictly contained in PAR_ℝ (respectively PAR_ℂ).

Both closed thresholds, $Y \le \mathrm{per}_m(X)$ and $Y \ge \mathrm{per}_m(X)$, also lie in PAR_ℝ and outside NP_ℝ, coNP_ℝ and Σ₂^ℝ. Negating one matrix row and the claimed value interchanges the thresholds. Their intersection is the graph.

The separations use the superpolynomial lower bound for the border complexity of the permanent (Theorem 2.3 in the paper, cited from the Anthropic manuscript "VP ≠ VNP over every field" and its Lean formalization). Two families of results use no lower bound:

- the graph theorems: a polynomial of polynomial degree whose graph has short existential, universal or ∃∀ real certificates has small border complexity;
- AM_ℝ[O(1)] = NP_ℝ ⊊ AM_ℝ[poly].

## Lean formalization

The directory `lean/` contains a formalization of the paper in Lean 4 (v4.36.0) with Mathlib (v4.36.0), about 36 000 lines. To check it:

```
cd lean
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

- Every numbered lemma, proposition, theorem and corollary of §2–§7, Theorems A–F, and Remarks 4.3, 5.4 and 7.4(a) are stated and proved, with no `sorry`. `#print axioms` reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` for every result.
- The results that the paper cites are explicit hypotheses of the theorems that use them, not axioms: In particular, every separation is conditional on `PermLowerBound`, exactly as in the paper.
  - SAFacts — facts (S2)–(S8) on semialgebraic sets;
  - PermLowerBound — Theorem 2.3, the border-complexity lower bound for the permanent;
  - BurgisserThm — Theorem 2.5;
  - EffectiveQE — effective quantifier elimination [BPR06];
  - CountingBound — the counting bound (C) of Lemma 7.7;
  - ClassFacts — the known inclusions between complexity classes.
- Fact (S1) holds by construction: semialgebraic sets are defined as first-order definable sets. Baur–Strassen, Ryser's formula, Hoeffding's inequality (from Mathlib) and the unrolling of machines into trees are proved.
- Appendix A of the paper (`sections/lean.tex`) and `lean/README.md` list the Lean name of each result, the modelling choices, and the few places where a formal proof differs from the text. `lean/ClassSeparations/Pinned.lean` restates the main statements verbatim and checks them against the proved theorems.

The proofs have not been peer reviewed. The paper is mostly AI-written (see above). The results are formalized in Lean, conditional on the cited results listed above.

The bibliographic data in `refs.bib` were checked against publisher and Crossref records. The numbered results cited from the Anthropic manuscript, Bürgisser (2004, 2026), Baartse–Meer, Baur–Strassen and Cucker et al. were confirmed in the sources. Still to be confirmed against the full texts: the attributions to Cucker (1992, 1993), Koiran (1994), Blum–Cucker–Shub–Smale, and the numbering in Basu–Pollack–Roy, which follows the second edition (2006).
