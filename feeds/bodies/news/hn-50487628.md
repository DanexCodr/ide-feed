Gradient-based design needs the derivative of an objective with respect to the parameters a designer edits, such as a hole radius or a fillet radius. In a boundary-representation (BRep) kernel those parameters pass through Boolean operations, filleting and tessellation, which make discrete choices. We freeze the tessellation’s topology at the current parameters and differentiate its node positions: surface nodes by evaluating the kernel’s analytic surfaces on dual numbers, vertices and trim nodes by implicit differentiation of the surface equations, with tangency rows for fillet blends. The result is exact for the frozen mesh, extends to directional second derivatives and to a transposed (reverse) pass, and feeds mass properties and a rigid-body adjoint with penalty contact. A change of topology is reported as an error.

We validate against finite differences of the frozen plan and of fresh rebuilds that share no tessellation with the seam, and state where the method falls short: the derivative of the frozen mesh differs from the continuum by the polygonization error (6.7% for a fillet at 16 segments per circle, 0.11% at 128), a plan stays valid for parameter steps of at least  mm, and the same-plan checks found two errors in the seam on filleted, drilled parts, which we describe and have fixed.

## 1 Introduction

This is not a new problem, and our approach is not new in outline. Engineering Sketch Pad computes **tessellation sensitivities** on a parametric BRep: the motion of every grid point with respect to every design parameter, on a tessellation whose topology is kept consistent between the base and perturbed models, with sensitivities found on Nodes, then Edges, then Faces [<sup>1</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-41)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>2</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-42). Those sensitivities are analytic for most construction operations and fall back to finite differences for others, filleting among them [<sup>1</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-41). Banović et al. differentiate the Open CASCADE kernel itself by operator overloading [<sup>3</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-43)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>4</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-44); algorithmic differentiation of that kind reaches every operation the kernel performs, fillets included, and can be nested for second derivatives, at the cost of differentiating the kernel’s combinatorial code and taping its execution.

We describe the same idea, which we call the **seam**, in vcad, our open-source Rust BRep kernel, and extend it in four directions. None is new to differentiation in general. What is new is having them in a frozen-tessellation method that leaves the kernel’s code undifferentiated.

- Analytic fillet sensitivities without AD or FD. Where Engineering Sketch Pad falls back to finite differences for fillets, a fillet radius moves every blend’s radius and axis at once, and its blends meet their support faces tangentially, which makes the implicit system at their corner vertices rank-deficient. We add tangency rows that complete it (Section 3.3), so one fillet parameter, moving 20 surfaces, is differentiated without finite differences.
- Second order on the frozen plan. Directional second derivatives of mass properties, with the node accelerations of cones and tori (Section 4).
- A transposed pass. Node velocities are linear in per-surface seed values; transposing those maps gives the derivative for every parameter from one pass (Section 5).
- Rigid-body dynamics. Mass-property and contact cotangents from a discrete trajectory adjoint pulled back through the seam (Section 7).

We validate the derivatives against central finite differences of the frozen plan and, wherever a rebuild keeps its topology, of rebuilds tessellated from scratch, which share nothing with the seam’s code ([Section 8](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-31)); the exceptions are listed there. We describe the two cases where the checks found errors in the seam. A script regenerates every measured value in this paper from a fresh clone ([Section 10](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-40)).

### 1.1 Scope and limits

- The derivative is that of the frozen mesh. The seam differentiates the mesh the kernel produces at , with segment counts and triangulation held fixed. It differs from the continuum shape derivative by the polygonization error, which falls as in the number of segments: for a filleted cube at 16 segments per full circle (four per quarter-blend) the fillet-radius derivative is 6.7% larger in magnitude than the continuum one, and 0.11% at 128 (Figure 8).
- A plan is valid only near where it was captured. Re-evaluating a plan matches every node to its capture-time position within mm. On the five-parameter box of Section 8.4, every parameter can move by at least mm, the largest step of our logarithmic grid that kept correspondence; the next grid point, mm, lost it. The optimizer therefore captures a fresh plan at every iterate.
- Segment counts can change between iterates. vcad’s Boolean rims are sag-adaptive: their arc count depends on the radius. Sweeping the box’s hole radius from 1.6 to 2.4 mm changes the node count 20 times, and each change moves the volume by up to of its value. Across iterates is therefore piecewise smooth with small jumps. The optimizer does not reject a step because its node count differs from the last iterate’s, because each iterate’s gradient is taken on its own plan; the jumps show up as noise in the line search.
- No derivatives across topology changes. When a perturbation would change the topology (a blind hole breaking through, a fillet consuming a face), the seam detects it with a topology signature and per-node correspondence checks and returns an error, and the optimizer shrinks the step (Figure 1).
- Seedings synthesized from the build function are not exact. They are central differences of surface fields, accurate to , and they cost two rebuilds per parameter (Section 6).
- Contact is phyz’s differentiable penalty model, a per-vertex spring–damper against a ground plane without friction, not a GJK/EPA collision pipeline <sup>5</sup>. The trajectory adjoint supports single-degree-of-freedom joints, open-loop control and objectives on the final state only.
- Everything runs on CPU in double precision.

## 2 Background and related work

**Parametric CAD sensitivities.** Structural shape optimization has long used design velocities, the sensitivity of boundary points to design variables [<sup>6</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-46)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>7</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-47). For CAD models these come from three routes. Finite differences of rebuilds with point correspondence are the most general and the most expensive; Robinson et al. and Agarwal et al. compute parametric design velocities this way for use with adjoint solvers [<sup>8</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-48)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>9</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-49). Differentiating the kernel by operator overloading gives exact derivatives of the kernel’s operations: Banović et al. and Mykhaskiv et al. differentiate Open CASCADE and couple it to a discrete adjoint CFD solver [<sup>3</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-43)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>4</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-44), and Xu et al. differentiate a NURBS-based design chain [<sup>10</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-50). Engineering Sketch Pad differentiates its own constructive solid geometry process analytically where it can and uses finite differences elsewhere, and maps the result onto a consistent tessellation [<sup>1</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-41)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>2</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-42). Our seam is closest to Engineering Sketch Pad: we leave the kernel’s combinatorial code undifferentiated and differentiate the map from parameters to a tessellation of fixed topology. Within that frozen-tessellation approach we add analytic fillet sensitivities, second derivatives, a transposed pass, and the coupling to rigid-body dynamics with contact; operator-overloading AD of a kernel can supply fillet and second derivatives too, by differentiating the kernel’s own code. Isogeometric analysis avoids the tessellation by computing on the CAD basis [<sup>11</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-51).

**Differentiable CAD programs.** Cascaval et al. write CAD programs in an automatically differentiable language for bidirectional editing, and do not optimize across changes of topology [<sup>12</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-52). Michel and Boubekeur compute parameter updates for direct manipulation of procedural shapes [<sup>13</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-53). Schulz et al. precompute a design space of CAD parameters for interactive optimization [<sup>14</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-54).

**Differentiable rendering and other representations.** Differentiable rasterizers [<sup>15</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-55)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>16</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-56) and path tracers [<sup>17</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-57) compute derivatives of images with respect to the vertices of a mesh of fixed connectivity, as ours is. Worchel and Alexa extract a mesh from parametric curves and surfaces for a differentiable mesh renderer and report that naive differentiable tessellation is fragile [<sup>18</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-58). Signed distance fields, learned [<sup>19</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-59) or analytic, are differentiable almost everywhere and can be rendered differentiably [<sup>20</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-60), and program synthesis recovers CSG trees from shapes [<sup>21</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-61)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>22</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-62). These representations give up the BRep’s exact faces, trims and fillets.

**Differentiable physics and co-design.** Differentiable simulators include rigid-body formulations with contact [<sup>23</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-63)<sup>–</sup>[<sup>25</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-64) and differentiable programming systems [<sup>26</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-65). Robot co-design work optimizes morphology and control jointly [<sup>27</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-66)<sup>,</sup>[<sup>28</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-67), usually over parametric primitives rather than CAD models. Placing a solver or a simulation inside a differentiable program as one step with a known adjoint is standard [<sup>29</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-68); our rollout coupling is such a step written by hand.

## 3 The differentiable seam

### 3.1 Frozen plans

vcad represents solids as BReps whose faces carry analytic surfaces (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, bilinear patch, B-spline), with Boolean operations, filleting and a tessellator. Two properties of the kernel shaped the design. First, its Boolean and fillet code is order-nondeterministic: two builds of the same model can enumerate isomorphic topology in different orders, flip a blend cylinder’s axis or rotate its reference direction, so traversal order cannot carry node identity from one build to another. Second, the stock mesh stores 32-bit positions, at which a central difference with  is meaningless, so the seam emits its own 64-bit mesh.

- Vertex nodes coincide with a topology vertex. Their velocity comes from implicit differentiation (Section 3.3).
- Surface nodes are interior to a face, stored as fixed surface coordinates . Their position is for the surface’s fields (offset, radius, axis, half-angle).
- Trim nodes lie on the intersection of two faces with non-parallel normals but are not topology vertices, for example a ring on a cylinder’s cap. Their position is the Newton solution of , with the intersection tangent, so a trim node follows the moving trim whichever surface carries .

## 5 The transposed pass and optimization

The optimizer is L-BFGS with memory 8 [<sup>32</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-71), box projection and an Armijo line search on the projected step. Each iterate rebuilds the part and captures a fresh plan. A trial step that fails with a topology or correspondence error counts as a failed step. A curvature pair is dropped when  or when its line search had to shrink past such an error, and a direction that is not a descent direction clears the memory.

## 9 Discussion

**Why freeze the tessellation.** Differentiating the kernel’s code has to give some answer at every branch: which face a Boolean keeps, how a fillet orders its blends, how many segments a circle gets. Those answers are piecewise constant in , and in vcad they are also order-nondeterministic. Freezing the plan makes the discrete state explicit and checkable. The cost is the limits of [Section 1.1](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-3): a plan is valid for steps of  mm on the five-parameter box, segment counts change between iterates, and the derivative is that of the mesh. For optimization this has been enough on every part we tried, because each iterate captures a new plan and the jumps between plans are of order  of the objective; for an objective that depends on fine surface detail, the polygonization gap would dominate before the jumps do.

**What the finite differences show.** On every plane, cylinder, cone and torus model, same-plan FD, rebuild FD and the seam agree. On filleted solids rebuild FD is noisy at small steps, because rebuilds place nodes differently, and has to be read at a step of  to  mm ([Figure 7](https://muni.works/research/vcad#fig:sweep)). At those steps it confirms the seam on the filleted cube and the filleted, drilled box. Same-plan FD is the sharper test, since it has no rebuild noise, and it is the one that exposed the two seam errors of [Section 8.4](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-34): each showed as an error that stayed fixed while the step moved over two decades.

**Toward an operator graph.** The rollout coupling of [Section 7](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-29) composes three steps by hand: the seam (parameters to node positions, with its transposed pass), mass properties (node positions to ten scalars per body) and the phyz adjoint (scalars and contact vertices to ). Each is a step with a forward map and an exact vector–Jacobian product, which is the shape of an operator node in a differentiable program [<sup>29</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-68). We are building that graph on the tang tensor compiler [<sup>35</sup>](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-74), where linear solves and simulations become nodes with their own adjoints; none of that is evaluated here.

**Remaining gaps.** Cone-tangent-to-plane tangency rows are not implemented; fillets with asymmetric corners or three blends at a vertex fall back to an older pipeline that is not differentiated; the contact model is frictionless; and the method is demonstrated only on vcad. Nothing in it depends on vcad beyond analytic surfaces that evaluate on dual numbers and a tessellator that can classify its nodes.

It then copies in `paper_experiments.rs` and `paper_contact_fillet.rs`, the experiments added for this paper (rebuild FD, step sweeps, resolution study, optimizer baselines, costs, plan radius, segment-count jumps, second-order sweeps, the flywheel L-BFGS run and the filleted-box contact rollout), runs everything, runs `irys.sh` for the rose-arm benchmark, and writes `data/measured.tsv`. This document reads every measured value, table cell and plotted point from that file, with two exceptions stated where they occur: the figures quoted from the earlier kernel revision `d10f279` ([Section 8.4](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-34)), and the correspondence and topology thresholds of the irys rebuild probe, which come from a separate probe run ([Section 8.8](https://muni.works/research/vcad#loc-37)). Model construction counts (segments requested, the 112-gon rim) are inputs, not measurements. The run took place on 2026-11-10T16:01Z on Linux 6.8.0-147-generic x86_64.

## 11 Conclusion

Freezing a BRep’s tessellation and differentiating its node positions gives derivatives of CAD parameters, fillet radii included, that are exact for the mesh, extend to second order and a transposed pass, and carry through to mass properties and rigid-body dynamics with contact. The approach follows Engineering Sketch Pad’s tessellation sensitivities; within that approach our additions are analytic fillet sensitivities, second derivatives, the transposed pass and the dynamics coupling. Its limits are those of the frozen mesh, a polygonization gap that falls as  and plans valid for small steps. Checking against finite differences of one frozen plan found two errors in the seam, both since fixed. vcad, the experiments and the data are open source.
