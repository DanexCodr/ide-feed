A blockbuster new investigation from [Groundwork Collaborative](https://groundworkcollaborative.org/) exposes how **Amazon shoppers may be getting hosed during the retail giant’s much-touted Black Friday and Cyber Monday sale events**. The report, *[Deal or Decoy? Amazon’s Deceptive Discounts](http://groundworkcollaborative.org/amazon)*, examines products the retailer showcased as some the “best” discounts of the year to shoppers hunting for bargains during these sale events. A closer look at hundreds of these “deals” finds that Amazon frequently offered shoppers the same price, or in some cases a higher price, during recent holiday sale events than they could have paid just weeks earlier.

**The report’s authors, Breyon Williams, Elizabeth Pancotti, and Lindsay Owens, find that nearly 1 in 3 of these Amazon “deals” offered no real savings** – the exact same products were sold for the same price or less in the month before the sale. **During Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales in the past two years, prices for nearly 1 in 10 products advertised as “deals” were *higher*** than what customers could have paid for the products in the month leading up to the sales.

Groundwork’s analysis examined the prices of more than 350 products included on Amazon’s own deal pages for the Black Friday and Cyber Monday sale events in 2024 and 2025. These pages promote these deals as some of the “best” deals available. Amazon’s Black Friday 2025 deals page says, “With so many deals to explore, we’ve handpicked some of the best ones you’ll find.”

**Groundwork Collaborative President and CEO and report co-author [Lindsay Owens](https://groundworkcollaborative.org/person/lindsay-owens/), shared the following reaction:**

“Millions of Americans wait all year for Black Friday and Cyber Monday to score the biggest bargains on stocking stuffers and household staples. Shoppers trust that the deals advertised to them deliver real savings because Amazon promotes them that way. But it turns out that these major sales aren’t all they’re cracked up to be. Instead, our investigation reveals that shoppers who wait for these major sale events may be playing rip-off roulette.

“Amazon dominates online shopping, and families count on its sale events to stretch their dollars. With the holiday shopping season upon us and prices continuing to rise, shoppers deserve to know they can trust every deal Amazon promises them. If Amazon can’t deliver that basic promise, lawmakers should hold them accountable.”

Owens is the author of the recent book *[Gouged: The End of a Fair Price—And What That Means for Your Wallet](https://gougedbook.com/)*, which exposes how corporations are sophisticated pricing schemes to overcharge consumers.

- The report’s key findings include:
  - The price of nearly 1 in 3 products that Amazon described as “deals” was the same – or higher – during sale events than what customers could have purchased them for in the month leading up to the sale.
  - Some Amazon “deals” offered shoppers higher prices than they could have paid before the sale.
    - On Black Friday 2025, a pair of yellow Hunter Women’s Short Rain Boots “on sale” for $126.99 had been $79.97 nine days before the sale event started, meaning customers looking for a deal could’ve paid 59% more if they waited for the sale.
    - On Cyber Monday 2025, a pair of men’s UGG Butte boots “on sale” for $179.98 had been $98.40 roughly four weeks before the start of the sale event, meaning the “deal” cost 83% more than the recent low.
    - Among these “worse” deals, the median sale-event price was 19% higher than what shoppers could have paid for the products in the weeks leading up to the sale.
- Since learning of our study, Amazon has removed the aforementioned deal pages our study drew from, but archived versions of these pages can be found at the following links: Black Friday 2024, Cyber Monday 2024, Black Friday 2025, Cyber Monday 2025.
- Amidst the ongoing affordability crisis, millions of Americans count on holiday sale events, like Black Friday, to deliver real savings. In 2025, a record 203 million Americans shopped between Thanksgiving and Cyber Monday, and this year, 70% of U.S. customers plan to take advantage of holiday promotions.
- *Deal or Decoy* calls on federal and state regulators to crack down on misleading discounts and require retailers to show the lowest price a product sold for in the previous 30 days when advertising a sale.

**ADDITIONAL BACKGROUND **

**The new report builds on Groundwork’s work exposing corporate pricing abuses and delivering results for American consumers.**

- In December 2025, Groundwork teamed up with Consumer Reports and More Perfect Union to expose how Instacart was charging shoppers different prices for the exact same groceries at the same store and time.
  - The findings made national headlines, triggered investigations by the Federal Trade Commission and New York Attorney General, and prompted Instacart to end the practice just weeks later.
- Following the publication of Groundwork’s Owens’ book Gouged: The End of a Fair Price—And What That Means for Your Wallet, the Federal Trade Commission launched an investigation into major retailers’ use of customers’ personal data to set prices, adding to growing scrutiny of corporate pricing practices and their impact on Americans’ wallets in response to Groundwork and Owens’ research.
- In October 2026, Groundwork exposed Walmart’s creepy patent filings and pricing playbook. The report digs into patent filings, privacy disclosures, and technology investments to reveal how the retailer is building the infrastructure that could allow it to deploy personalized pricing at scale.
