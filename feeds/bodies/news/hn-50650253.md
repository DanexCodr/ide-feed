Authorities are ramping up efforts to manage a fire ant nest in Sydney after investigations revealed a larger and more complex infestation than first identified.

A nest detected in Auburn was successfully treated at the weekend, but further surveillance has revealed a more extensive presence of fire ants requiring intensive treatment and a broader biosecurity response.

It comes after Commonwealth authorities detected the insects, known for their painful sting, at the Enfield Intermodal Logistics Centre, in Sydney's inner west, on September 23.

Imported goods are suspected to be the source of both incursions, which are not otherwise believed to be connected.

NSW Agriculture Minister Tara Moriarty said a task force was being established to determine the ants' origins.

"The experts believe that they came in through containers that have come in likely from China," she said.

"At this stage, they are still only on this particular site in Auburn, but we will undertake further surveillance … to see if they have spread.

"I was able to check [the Auburn site] out myself yesterday, and even though the area is not geographically big, the types of ants that we have here do spread in a different way than what we've seen ants do in New South Wales."

Ms Moriarty said the Auburn infestation was located in an industrial area and included businesses that imported goods from Asia and elsewhere.

## Management efforts underway

A biosecurity order is expected to be issued by Friday to regulate the movement of high-risk materials through Auburn, including soils, turf, mulch and building materials.

Additional biosecurity officers and detection dogs will also be deployed as part of the response.

Ms Moriarty said there had been more than 900 reports of suspicious nests from the public recent days, and asked locals to remain on the lookout for infestations.

> "Biosecurity is everybody's responsibility,"

The ants are up to 6 millimetres long, dark reddish-brown with brown-black abdomens and are known to be aggressive.

Bites that have a burning sensation can cause itchy blisters or pustules and lead to the hospitalisation of children and death in severe cases.

If spotted, the nests must not be destroyed and members of the public are urged not to try to capture the ants, which are classified as prohibited matter in NSW.

Suspected fire ants can be reported to the Biosecurity Helpline on 1800 680 244.
