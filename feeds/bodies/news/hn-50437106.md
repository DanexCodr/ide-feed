This notice is about the security incident at one of our vendors, Fireworks AI, that may impact some Notion workspaces. For the latest on the Fireworks AI incident, see their [official security updates here](https://fireworks.ai/security/bulletins/october-2026-security-incident-update).

Following an investigation by the Notion security team, we have determined that <1% of Notion workspaces may have been affected by the Fireworks security incident. Some unattributable chat sessions with Notion AI may have been exposed in this incident. “Unattributable” means the chats weren't labeled with who wrote them or which workspace they came from, and the transcripts did not contain tool call results. Beyond what’s in the chat transcript itself, there’s no information tying the chat back to a workspace, user, or company. No other data from within the workspace is affected.

#### **What is Fireworks AI and how we used them**

Fireworks is one of the most-used global AI infrastructure platforms powering tens of thousands of customers, and used by hundreds of thousands of developers. We used Fireworks for classification jobs to help customers understand their own AI usage through a feature in Notion called [Insights](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-ai-settings-overview#insights), and we used them as a general inference provider to serve open-weight models inside our product. The Insights feature has been disabled as we investigate this incident, and we are no longer serving open-weight models through Fireworks.

#### **What happened and how we addressed it**

We learned from Fireworks about potential incidents earlier this month, and we shut down all connections to their systems out of an abundance of caution.

On November 6, Fireworks notified us that malicious actors may have accessed potential customer data. Our team immediately began another internal investigation. On November 8, Fireworks confirmed this new dataset for us.

Upon analysis of the new data, we discovered that it included unattributable Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature. Chats weren't labeled with who wrote them or which workspace they came from, and the transcripts did not contain any tool call results. Beyond the words in the chat transcripts themselves, the data contained no information that could specifically identify the workspace, user, or company.

No other Notion data from your workspace is affected, and we continue to monitor this incident.

What does “unattributable” mean? Could the conversations still contain sensitive information?

Following an investigation by the Notion security team, we have determined that <1% of Notion workspaces may have been affected by the Fireworks security incident. Some unattributable chat sessions with Notion AI may have been exposed.

"Unattributable" means the chats weren't labeled with who wrote them or which workspace they came from. The transcripts also did not contain tool call results (e.g., Notion page content, or external data pulled in by Notion AI.) Beyond what’s in the chat transcript itself, there’s no information tying the chat back to specific workspaces, users, or companies. No other data from within your workspace is affected.

As a precaution, we recommend that you treat this unattributable data as if it were exposed. If anyone on your team pasted sensitive information like passwords, API keys, or other credentials into Notion AI during this timeframe, we recommend rotating them right away.

How did Notion use Fireworks with the Learning and Early Access Program (LEAP)?

Customers in the [Learning and Early Access Program](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-ai-leap-program-faqs) (LEAP) volunteer their data to help Notion test new AI technology. In the past, we used this volunteered data to experiment with training task-specific models on Fireworks.
