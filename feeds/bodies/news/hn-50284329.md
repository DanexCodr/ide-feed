*Our live coverage of Game 5 of the World Series has ended. For a full recap of the Brewers’ World Series-clinching victory, read below.*

The Milwaukee Brewers put on a masterclass in every facet of the game as they beat the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 to win their first World Series title in franchise history in five games on Wednesday at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Florida.

In a game that saw both teams’ aces go head-to-head, it was the Brewers’ Cy Young candidate Jacob Misiorowski besting Rays ace Drew Rasmussen.

The NL Cy Young frontrunner went five-and-two-thirds innings, striking out two while only allowing two hits before exiting the game with an apparent cramp in his leg. Rasmussen was pulled after four innings with him being on short rest and a reported pitch count of 60.

The real story was the Brewers offense – especially rookie shortstop Cooper Pratt.

Pratt got the scoring started in the second inning with an RBI single, scoring Brice Turang. And Milwaukee never looked back.

That wasn’t the only notable thing Pratt did – he made a little World Series history with his single. It was his sixth straight at-bat with a hit, the most for a rookie in Fall Classic history.

But it was the sixth inning when things went up a notch. William Contreras’ RBI double scored a run and then he would score on Pratt’s sacrifice fly to build the lead to 4-0.

The Rays bullpen was able to settle in, using seven total pitchers, but the Tampa offense was sound asleep until Liam Hicks and Chandler Simpson both doubled in the ninth for the Rays, making the score 4-1.

Contreras was named the World Series MVP after batting .381 with four RBI.

The Brewers will now bring a World Series title back to Wisconsin.

For the first time in franchise history, the Milwaukee Brewers are World Series champions.

The Brew Crew dominated the Tampa Bay Rays in Game 5, winning 4-1 in St. Petersburg, Florida.

## Brewers closer: '10-year-old me would be so proud'

It’s a kid’s game played by grown men. But sometimes it’s nice to see that those men don’t lose sight of what it all means.

Trevor Megill closed out the game for the Brewers, getting the final six outs to clinch the World Series – a scenario that he dreamt about over and over.

“For the last two months or so, this is everything I could think of,” Megill told Fox. “You know, going to bed, coming to the field every single day. This moment right here.”

With a championship belt slung over his shoulder, Megill said: “This is just incredible, man. Ten-year-old me would be so proud.”

Brewers owner Mark Attanasio credited the city of Milwaukee and Brewers fans for their title win.

“Milwaukee has waited 44 years and Milwaukee you have your world championship,” Attanasio shouted.

Attanasio’s last words before celebrating more? “GO BREWERS!”

The Brewers’ star catcher William Contreras was named the MVP of the World Series, a well-deserved honor.

Contreras was excellent defensively behind the plate and made great use of his team Automatic Ball-Strike System challenges. He batted .381, knocked in four runs and was the spark plug for the team all series long.

“I don’t have words to say for how I’m feeling but I am just appreciative of my teammates. Appreciative of (Pat Murphy). I thank all of them,” Contreras said via his translator.

Contreras also called up star reliever Aaron Ashby, who was extremely complimentary of the catcher.

“He wants to compete every single day. He’d die for everyone on this team and he’d do anything for any of the guys on this team. He’s our bulldog and we love this dude,” Ashby said.

Milwaukee has waited 57 years for a World Series title. Cooper Pratt had to wait about four months.

Pratt, the Brewers shortstop who caught fire in this World Series at the plate, was called up to the Brewers in June. He found himself starting at shortstop in the Fall Classic just a few short months later and is now a champion.

“This is one of the greatest moments of my life. Oh my gosh,” he said, overwhelmed by the moment. “It doesn’t even feel real. Like, I don’t know. This is this is surreal. Only took four months!”

Pratt thanked Brewers fans and his teammates for being patient with him as he developed in the big leagues all season.

“The pressure is out of this world. It’s some of the hardest but most fun baseball I’ve ever been a part of,” he said. “I messed up a lot of times this postseason, but the guys picked. up, and we’re here, and we’re world champions. It’s so special, man. I’m so blessed.”

Bob Uecker spent 54 seasons as the voice of the Milwaukee Brewers, narrating every game every summer until his death in January 2025.

His loss was keenly felt in Wisconsin and throughout baseball as he was one of the game’s most well-known personalities.

Uecker died without ever seeing the Brewers win a World Series title, but Christian Yelich said he had a feeling that Ueck was watching on Wednesday.

“He’s smiling, man. He’s smiling. I really wish he could have been here to see this,” Yelich told the Fox broadcast. “But like we said, a bunch – he’s been with us a lot and we keep him close to our hearts. And you know, this one’s still for Bob. This is for Ueck.”

Ace and NL Cy Young award frontrunner Jacob Misiorowski was emotional after helping lead the Brewers to their first title in franchise history.

“I don’t even have words,” Misiorowski told the Fox broadcast. “You know, it’s amazing. Just watching my guys celebrate. In the city, I know they’re going crazy in Milwaukee. It’s awesome.”

Miz gave props to his catcher William Contreras for helping him handle the emotions throughout the season.

“We’ve been talking this whole week, and expecting this moment. … I couldn’t have done it without him, and it’s been amazing with him this whole season,” Misiorowski said.

Christian Yelich’s arrival in Milwaukee in 2018 marked a turning point for the franchise, making him the centerpiece of the next eras of Brewers baseball.

Yelich won the MVP that year and remained one of the biggest parts of the team, even as injuries robbed him of some of the production he had earlier in his career. With this title, he finally gets to call himself a World Series champion.

“I don’t even know how to describe it. It’s the best feeling ever. All of our fans are here,” Yelich told the Fox broadcast. “I know the city’s waited a long, long time for this, and we’re so happy to bring it home to them.

“Can’t wait to get back there and celebrate. I wish everybody could experience what this feels like. It feels amazing.”

## The Milwaukee Brewers are World Series champions!

The Milwaukee Brewers have erased nearly six decades of heartache, defeating the Tampa Bay Rays in Game 5 to clinch the franchise’s first World Series title.

The Brewers used their small-ball ways, leaning on clutch hitting, stellar defense and exquisite pitching from ace Jacob Misiorowski to claim Game 5 and win the title. It was a fitting exhibition of Brewers baseball that carried them to baseball’s best record in the regular season and saw them roll through the postseason.

RBI singles from Cooper Pratt and David Hamilton built the lead in the second and fifth innings, but it was the sixth when things went up a notch. William Contreras’ RBI double scored a run and then he would score on Pratt’s sacrifice fly to build the lead to 4-0.

Liam Hicks and Chandler Simpson both doubled in the ninth for the Rays, making the score 4-1.

It’s an incredible moment for the city of Milwaukee’s baseball fans. The Brewers had only been to one World Series before this year, losing in seven games to the St. Louis Cardinals in 1982. After coming within three outs of the title on Tuesday and falling short, the Brewers will get to go back to Milwaukee for a victory parade rather than Game 6.

The Brewers’ win is a victory for the league’s smallest media market and 19th-biggest payroll. Notably, the Brewers swept the big-money Los Angeles Dodgers to get to the World Series, a major bit of payback for a franchise has been building toward this moment for a decade.
