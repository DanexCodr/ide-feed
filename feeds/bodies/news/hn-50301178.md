On 9 May 2021, I tagged release 0.1.0 of *OqtaDrive*, simply titled “first alpha release”. It was a hobby project that had sat on the shelf for a while, until discussions over at *World of Spectrum* revived it: an *Arduino Nano* pretending to be a bank of eight *Sinclair Microdrives*, for the *ZX Spectrum* with *Interface 1* as well as the *QL*. Five years and 34 releases later, time for a look back.

#### Microdrives First

The core idea hasn’t changed since day one. The *Nano* is just a protocol converter. With only 2KB of RAM, it can’t hold a 144KB cartridge, so it continuously streams the data over a 1Mb/s serial link to a daemon written in *Go*, running on your PC or a *RaspberryPi Zero*. The daemon does the heavy lifting, and that’s where most new features landed ([more about the beginnings](https://oqtadrive.org/09.background/development/)).

2021 was about getting the basics solid: auto detection of *Spectrum* and *QL*, co-existing with real *Microdrives*, an installer, and a first web UI. Thanks to Tom Dalby open-sourcing *Z80onMDR*, snapshot files (and later *TAP* files) can be loaded directly into a virtual drive. Tom also designed several PCBs and 3D printed cases, from a tiny micro version to a standalone unit with a *Pi* on board. In October 2021, *OqtaDrive* went on display at the *Vintage Computing Festival Berlin*.

The following years brought the move to *Codeberg*, ready-made setup images, upgrades from the browser, controlling *OqtaDrive* from the *Spectrum* or *QL* itself, searching and loading software straight from [*ZXDB*](https://zxinfo.dk), a file system implementation for editing cartridges, and even *CP/M* support.

#### Then Came the Network

The *Interface 1* and the *QL* don’t only talk to *Microdrives*. They also come with the *Sinclair Network*, a simple LAN from the early 80s. With release 0.7.0 in April 2024, *OqtaDrive* became an *Internet* gateway for it. Add one transistor and four resistors to the adapter, connect to a hub server, and a friend on the other side of the world can `LOAD *"n";18` what you just sent with `SAVE *"n";24`. No modifications, no extra software on the vintage machine.

Since then, private on-demand networks were added, also a flat addressing scheme for network games, chat and echo services on the hub, and with 0.8.4, a major round of stability fixes under heavy load. Big thanks to Hans Kramp for finding that issue and relentlessly stress testing the fix!
