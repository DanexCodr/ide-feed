School cellphone bans are beginning to modestly improve students’ academic performance, according to the first large national analysis that compared students’ test scores before and after the bans.

Schools with strict bans — requiring students to lock their phones in [pouches](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/02/25/style/yondr-pouch-school-phone-ban.html) all school day — saw math and reading scores improve as if students had an additional week or two of school a year, [the study](https://cepr.harvard.edu/phone-free-schools) found. It was published Tuesday by the Center for Education Policy Research at Harvard.

U.S. students’ test scores [began falling over a decade ago](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/13/upshot/test-scores-school-districts-us.html). The drop coincided with the rise of smartphones, raising questions about their role in the decline. The evidence so far on the academic effects of phone bans [has been mixed](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/04/us/did-school-cellphone-bans-study.html). But the new study indicates they are starting to have an influence.

“The earlier studies on cellphone bans have been dismissed as implying small impacts, and in-school cellphone use has been ruled out as playing a big role in the decline,” said Thomas Kane, a professor at the Harvard Graduate School of Education and an author of the study. “But we think that’s mistaken.”

The study looked at 3.3 million students between 2021 and 2026. They were in third through 10th grade, attending 4,600 U.S. schools. Using scores on [MAP tests](https://www.nwea.org/), which track achievement from fall to spring, it compared the performance of students at schools that put in strict phone bans with those at similar schools that did not have such bans.

The annual gains reversed about half the annual loss that an average student experienced in the years leading up to the pandemic. (Students lost even more ground during pandemic closures.)
