A taxpayer emailed consumer advocate Louis Rossmann a screenshot of the "UPDATED TERMS & CONDITIONS" screen the taxpayer said they saw while signing in to IRS.gov through ID.me, showing ID.me & IRS logos and one button, "Accept Terms & Continue".

ID.me's Privacy Policy dated November 2, 2026 says by using the platform "you agree that ID.me. determines how personal information may be used" for the user's wallet.<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup> The policy's May 7, 2026 text lacked it.<sup>[[12]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-oct2026-12)</sup>

The current Terms, Version 10.1.0 dated November 13, 2026, count continued use after revised Terms take effect as "acceptance of those changes", "Unless we expressly state otherwise"; a user who disagrees may stop using the Service & close the wallet first.<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup> Version 10.0.3, dated March 3, 2023, said continuing use after notice "will indicate your agreement to all changes", "Unless otherwise noted by us".<sup>[[13]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-2023-13)</sup> The Credential Policy, Version 10.1, published July 1, 2024, requires users to reaffirm acceptance of "the prevailing Terms of Service, Privacy and other Policies and any other required agreements" "at least once every 5 years" or face credential suspension.<sup>[[14]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-credential-policy-14)</sup>

Public radio program *[Marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketplace_(radio_program))* reported that Nancy Kim, a professor emerita at Illinois Tech's Chicago-Kent College of Law, said she found nothing reassuring in ID.me's terms, pointing out that although they state ID.me won't sell data, ID.me can change them.<sup>[[15]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-marketplace-15)</sup>

### Marketing use

ID.me's Privacy Policy says:

> We may use information for marketing purposes. We may use your information to send promotional messages and newsletters via email, or otherwise alert you to products or Services we think might be of interest to you, including for ID.me Shop.<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup>

ID.me Shop, a page of deals & discounts, says it "is part of the Amazon Associates program and other affiliate programs" & "may earn money when you buy products through links on Shop".<sup>[[5]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-shop-5)</sup>

The policy also says:

> We may share your information in connection with a corporate transaction. We may disclose or transfer your information as part of, or during negotiations for, any purchase, sale, lease, merger, or any other type of acquisition, disposal, or financing involving our brands.<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup>

A capitalized notice atop ID.me's Terms of Service says they contain "A MUTUAL ARBITRATION PROVISION, JURY TRIAL WAIVER, AND CLASS-ACTION WAIVER" & that users may opt out "WITHIN THIRTY (30) DAYS OF ACCEPTING THESE TERMS".<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup> Section 20 covers disputes "arising out of or relating to" the Terms, the Privacy Policy, the website or the service.<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup> A dispute not resolved within 45 calendar days after the notice of dispute is received goes to [arbitration](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/Forced_arbitration), where "a neutral arbitrator will resolve all Disputes"; both sides give up suing before a judge or jury, individually or in a class action, except in small claims court.<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup> Subject to an age exception, a valid opt-out & "nonwaivable statutory rights", disputes proceed "solely on an individual basis".<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup>

A user may opt out by emailing a notice titled "Opt-Out Notice" to [[email protected]](https://consumerrights.wiki/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection) within 30 calendar days "after you first accept these Terms" or within 30 calendar days "after a material change to the Opt-Out Provisions takes effect".<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup> Section 10 caps ID.me's aggregate liability at $1,000.00 "To the extent allowed by law".<sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup>

Version 10.0.3, dated March 3, 2023, had the same cap, & its notice listed "A BINDING ARBITRATION CLAUSE AND A CLASS ACTION WAIVER" with no opt-out.<sup>[[13]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-2023-13)</sup> The opt-out appears in Version 10.1.0, in a copy last updated September 18, 2025 whose opt-out text matches the November 13, 2026 version.<sup>[[16]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-sep2025-16)</sup><sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup>

Closing a wallet directs ID.me to deactivate the credential & "purge the associated data from active use", but ID.me "does retain" account & verification history "for up to three (3) years".<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup> Use of the credential with state or federal government agencies "will be stopped" if the account is deleted.<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup> Information given "in connection with a public sector agency" for verification "may be retained for up to three (3) years after wallet closure", "unless applicable regulations require a shorter retention period".<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup>

ID.me reserves the right to keep data from "certain high-risk transactions, particularly in government and healthcare settings", "exclusively for fraud prevention and government audit purposes".<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup> The policy says biometric deletion requests "may be subject to an internal investigation prior to completion", "where determined as necessary to protect the platform against fraud", followed by "ID.me reserves the right to decline such requests".<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup>

*Marketplace* reported that Kim said ID.me can keep a user's data for up to three years even after a deletion request.<sup>[[15]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-marketplace-15)</sup>

ID.me's Privacy Policy adds after its marketing clause:

> Please note, if you are using ID.me Services in connection with legal identity verification for a state or federal government agency, or in association with Electronic Prescriptions for Controlled Substance Services, ID.me does not use any Personal Information provided as part of your verification for any type of marketing or promotional purposes related to ID.me Shop without your consent, or unless you otherwise use your ID.me credential for verification with any ID.me customer who is not a state or federal government agency customer, use your ID.me wallet in connection with ID.me Shop, ID.me Jobs, or ID.me Rx, or otherwise opt-in to receiving marketing communications from ID.me.<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup>

ID.me's policy says the company "may share" a user's name, date of birth & contact details for reporting with the government agency the user verifies for, & that verifying "constitutes consent" to it.<sup>[[4]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-privacy-4)</sup> Terms of Service 10.1.0, dated September 18, 2025 & November 13, 2026, "do not provide consent to marketing calls or text messages".<sup>[[16]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-sep2025-16)</sup><sup>[[3]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-idme-terms-3)</sup>

The August 18, 2026 IRS help page said ID.me "will not share, sell, rent or trade" personally identifiable information "with other parties, except to verify your identity and eligibility";<sup>[[18]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-help-aug2026-18)</sup> the October 29, 2026 version points readers to "their privacy policies at Login.gov and IDMe".<sup>[[2]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-create-account-2)</sup>

The IRS help page last reviewed or updated on October 29, 2026 says that "The U.S. government maintains the Login.gov system", & that taxpayers who cannot verify their identity with one provider should "try the other provider".<sup>[[2]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-create-account-2)</sup> The version last reviewed or updated on August 18, 2026 named only ID.me as the sign-in account.<sup>[[18]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-help-aug2026-18)</sup> The IRS sign-in page says to "choose ID.me" for anyone who does not have "an SSN, U.S. address, and smartphone, or tablet with a camera", SSN being a Social Security number.<sup>[[1]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-signin-1)</sup>

Login.gov is provided by the federal [General Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Services_Administration) (GSA).<sup>[[19]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-logingov-rules-19)</sup> Login.gov's privacy page says a user who creates an account & verifies their identity consents to Login.gov "collecting, using, keeping, and sharing your personal information" as its policies describe, "including to verify your identity and to help detect and prevent fraud".<sup>[[20]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-logingov-policy-20)</sup>

Login.gov's Rules of Use say customer-support feedback may be used "for other purposes as we see fit, such as for marketing", though Login.gov "will not reveal your personal information outside of the Login.gov service" except "as required by applicable law or as stated elsewhere in these terms".<sup>[[19]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-logingov-rules-19)</sup> They also say disputes "shall be governed by federal law" & that no fees are charged for registering or using the service.<sup>[[19]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-logingov-rules-19)</sup> In a 2026 blog post, GSA wrote that Login.gov's privacy protections let people access government services "without their information being used for commercial purposes".<sup>[[21]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-gsa-blog-21)</sup>

Direct Pay, the IRS service for paying taxes from a bank account, lists "No sign-in required"; people who have never filed, or who last filed over six years ago, are told they "can pay another way".<sup>[[6]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-direct-pay-6)</sup> Taxpayers who cannot register online can have a tax return or tax account transcript mailed; the IRS lists "transcripts of past tax returns" & "tax account information" among their tax records.<sup>[[22]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-irs-transcript-22)</sup>

ID.me became available for TreasuryDirect logins on September 13, 2026 & replaced the traditional TreasuryDirect login on October 28, 2026.<sup>[[7]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-treasurydirect-faq-7)</sup> TreasuryDirect says Login.gov "cannot be used to log in to your TreasuryDirect account", & a user who declines to let ID.me share their verified identity information cannot log in.<sup>[[7]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-treasurydirect-faq-7)</sup> Its FAQ states neither TreasuryDirect nor ID.me "sells, rents, or shares your personal information with third parties for marketing or advertising purposes".<sup>[[7]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-treasurydirect-faq-7)</sup> An ID.me spokesman wrote to *Marketplace*, "We do not sell, rent, or trade users’ personal information".<sup>[[15]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-marketplace-15)</sup>

On October 29, 2026, [Senate Finance Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Finance) Ranking Member Ron Wyden, D-Ore., & [Senate Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Banking,_Housing,_and_Urban_Affairs) Ranking Member Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., demanded that Treasury halt "a rushed transition" to ID.me for Americans trying to access their savings bonds online.<sup>[[8]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-senate-release-8)</sup> The senators wrote:

> Americans should not have to provide sensitive personal information to ID.me as the price of accessing essential government services when a secure government-operated alternative is available<sup>[[8]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-senate-release-8)</sup>

They asked the agencies to delay the ID.me requirement until after the 2027 tax filing deadline.<sup>[[8]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-senate-release-8)</sup>

Their letter, dated October 27, 2026, said federal law requires agencies to implement "a single sign-on trusted identity platform" developed by the General Services Administration, citing 6 U.S.C. § 1523(b)(1)(D).<sup>[[23]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-wyden-warren-letter-23)</sup> It said ID.me was awarded a five-year agreement with a ceiling of more than $1 billion for identity verification, authentication, and fraud prevention services.<sup>[[23]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-wyden-warren-letter-23)</sup> The letter requested a response by November 9, 2026.<sup>[[23]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-wyden-warren-letter-23)</sup>

Trade publication Biometric Update reported that two Treasury orders for ID.me subscriptions were placed under "a five-year Treasury blanket purchase agreement for ID.me identity verification and authentication services" with "a ceiling of approximately $1.03 billion", and that "Federal procurement records show about $93 million obligated against the vehicle so far".<sup>[[24]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-biometric-update-24)</sup>

An August 31, 2026 memorandum from the [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget), M-26-18, says agencies offering other identity solutions must promote Login.gov as the default option for new account creation "for any user population that Login.gov is able to serve".<sup>[[25]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-omb-m-26-18-25)</sup> The IRS said in an emailed statement, quoted by news site FedScoop, that "Taxpayers can use Login.gov or ID.me" ahead of the 2027 filing season.<sup>[[26]](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/ID.me_terms_required_for_IRS_sign-in#cite_note-fedscoop-26)</sup>
