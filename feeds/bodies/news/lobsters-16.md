We’re announcing, and honestly celebrating, the next chapter in our push for memory safety: Sovereign Tech Agency have selected us as their partner for helping critical free and open source (FOSS) projects transition to memory safe tech like Rust, as part of their Sovereign Tech Resilience Program. In this article, we’ll go over the Program, why now is the right time to transition, and we’ll explain the basics of how FOSS projects can benefit from this collaboration, free of charge.

## The Resilience Program

The Sovereign Tech Agency are well-known in the FOSS ecosystem for their [Sovereign Tech Fund](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/fund), which does “strategic investments in the digital infrastructure of our economy and society”. In September 2026, they relaunched the [Sovereign Tech Resilience Program](https://www.sovereign.tech/news/resilience-relaunch), which works a little differently; STA has contracted suppliers to perform various services for FOSS projects, to strengthen the resilience of these projects. Unlike many grant programs in the industry, the Sovereign Tech Resilience Program does not give money directly to projects; instead it provides support from a paid-for engineering team.So how does this program strengthen the resilience of FOSS projects? By “improving projects’ underlying capacity to prevent, withstand, and respond to [bugs]” in various ways (which you can read more about [here](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/resilience)), including by offering support for transitioning code bases to Rust, Go, or Python.Transition to memory safetyThat is where we come in. We’ve been contracted to support participating projects in transitioning codebases, whole or in part, from memory-unsafe languages such as C or C++ to memory-safe alternatives where appropriate.The services we will be delivering to FOSS projects are:assessing which components are suitable for rewritesproducing prioritized migration roadmapscarrying out rewrites while maintaining functional equivalence and compatibilitytraining maintainers to keep developing the memory-safe componentsWhy nowA lot of our world depends on various critical open source projects. By now, it’s obvious that hardening these critical open source components, by investing in them and contributing to them, is important. As we’re currently seeing a surge in both (agentic) cyber attacks and AI-assisted vulnerability discoveries, this has become more pressing than ever before, and we’re not expecting this urgency to let up.There are, thankfully, different ways to go about it, and many excellent initiatives (e.g. [the Alpha Omega project](https://openssf.org/community/alpha-omega/)), but here’s why we’re so excited to be a part of this one specifically:The Programs’s criteria ensure a risk and impact based investmentA project’s maintainers stay in the leadThe effort is substantial enough (i.e. secured for at least 3 years) to create real and lasting impactTo us, being STA’s partner in this also presents a very tangible step in our advocacy for memory safety, which started in 2023 but has felt more pertinent each year since.How to apply for this programFor FOSS projects that are interested, the process is very straightforward:Read the [participation criteria](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/resilience/criteria)[**Apply to the program**](https://apply.sovereign.tech/)Get selectedMake a plan with STA and us, while you stay in the leadWe collaboratively execute the planYou can apply for one or multiple areas of support (aside from memory safety, think e.g. software supply chain security, security audits, and [more](https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/resilience)). The resulting engagement can be large or small, long or short, depending on the services you’re interested in and that fit your project’s needs.Questions?We are currently working on several FOSS projects that are unrelated to the Resilience Program.
