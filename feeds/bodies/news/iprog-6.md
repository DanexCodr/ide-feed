Meet BlimpMate, described as a flying display robot designed for calm and persistent interaction in indoor environments. Presented earlier this month at the ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology (UIST 2026) in Detroit, BlimpMate acts as an embodied AI assistant that brings digital information into physical 3D space without relying on fixed or handheld screens.  VIDEO Researchers Henghao Li, Shan Lin and colleagues explain that, rather than using fixed or handheld screen, flying displays offer an alternative that can position information near the user and the task at hand. The drawback of existing aerial interfaces which rely on multi-rotor drones is not only their noise and limited endurance, and also their exposed propellers. To overcome these problems they have created BlimpMate which, built on helium blimp platform that provides passive buoyant lift, offers quiet and energy-efficient operation while integrating a lightweight projection-based display, multi-modal sensing, custom low-level flight control, and a networked interaction stack with AI-supported services. So what possible use cases are there for BlimpMate in this era of AI Chatbots? Beyond the proof-of-concept demos in the paper, it is easy to imagine practical everyday uses:

#### Home Kitchen Sous-Chef

While you are busy chopping vegetables or managing multiple pots on the stove, your hands are messy and you shouldn't be touching a phone or tablet. BlimpMate floats quietly near head height beside the counter. You casually glance up and ask for the next step of a complex recipe. The blimp gently shifts forward and projects a clear, step-by-step ingredient list and live timer right in front of you. When a timer finishes, it displays a visual alert and offers to set the oven temperature, allowing you to stay completely focused on cooking hands-free.

#### Workshop Assistant

During a detailed electronics soldering or DIY crafting session in a home workshop, you need to reference a circuit schematic or measure a component. Instead of walking over to a desktop monitor or squinting at a workbench screen covered in tools, you summon BlimpMate via a voice prompt. It hovers directly into your field of view, displaying the precise wiring diagram or assembly guide at eye level. If you move to the other side of the workbench to check a different tool, the blimp smoothly repositions itself to maintain the optimal viewing angle without cluttering your physical workspace.

#### Collaborative Brainstorming

In a shared modern office or studio space, a team is brainstorming around a large table. Instead of crowding around a laptop or casting to a distant wall screen that blocks conversation, BlimpMate acts as a mobile, floating focal point. It can hover above the center of the discussion to display shared diagrams, meeting notes, or incoming video call feeds. Because it operates silently on passive helium lift rather than noisy drone rotors, it doesn't disrupt ongoing conversation or break the calm, collaborative atmosphere of the room.
