More than a decade after **"A Man From the Future",** the collaboration between the Pet Shop Boys and biographer Andrew Hodges about Alan Turing, originally debuted, a reworked and reorchestrated version is out on Parlophone Records. This musical composition includes songs, spoken word and electronic and orchestral music. It recounts the life and death of Alan Turing,the pioneering British mathematician and cryptanalyst who laid the foundations of modern computing but tragically took his own life after suffering chemical castration after a conviction for homosexuality. Neil Tennant and Chris Lowe originally wrote the work with Andrew Hodges back in 2012-13, the centenary of Alan Turing's birth. It was performed with the BBC Concert Orchestra and the BBC Singers at a late-night BBC Prom on July 23rd 2014, Turing's 102nd birthday, at the Royal Albert Hall in London. The revised version, is more intimate arrangement for eight string players and with more of an electronic emphasis. Alongside music and lyrics by Pet Shop Boys is a narration, spoken by actress Juliet Stevenson, based on Andrew Hodges' book **_Alan Turing: the Enigma_**. According to the Pet Shop Boys: _"Our motive in creating this work was to make a musical memorial to Alan Turing who gave so much to his country and the world and was rewarded for it by being prosecuted for his sexuality. Alan made a massive contribution to the defeat of Germany in World War II and was a pioneer of computing, imagining a world with intelligent computers. He also looked forward to a time when being gay would be accepted as normal. In both his professional and private lives, he was ahead of his time."_ In the eight tracks of _**A Man From the Future,**_ Turing's story unfolds chronologically from his school days, with his initial interest in mathematics and science and his realisation of his sexuality, to his university days at Cambridge, his leading role working for the British Government unlocking the German Enigma machine's encrypted messages during World War II, his place in the invention of the digital computer, his prediction of Artificial Intelligence, his trial and conviction for a homosexual relationship, and then his suicide in 1954. The last track features a passage recorded by former Prime Minister Gordon Brown from his 2009 statement acknowledging Turing's ground-breaking work in helping the UK win the war and apologising for the inhumane treatment he received when he was convicted under homophobic laws, which is followed by the posthumous royal pardon granted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2013. The final words evoke the significance of his work for today's digital world, and leave the listener with questions for the future. [](<https://amzn.to/4iJFqTH>)
