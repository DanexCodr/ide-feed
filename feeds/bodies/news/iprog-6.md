This is something that has never happened before — an ISO draft for C++ 26 has been rejected by a vote of the ISO committee. The divisive issue is a fundamental feature supposed to make C++ safe - contracts. Something the creator of C++ really doesn't like either. C++ is perhaps the most complex language of all time. It has everything including the kitchen sink and there are so many ways of doing things it cannot be regarded as a single language, but a collection of personal dialects trying to stick together. Cutting through this mess there is one clear voice— that of its originator, Bjarne Stroustrup, who always manages to put the best face on his language. So much so that it is difficult to understand why the committee doesn't just listen to him or, better still, stand assign and let him guide the process of improving the language. Contracts allow preconditions, postconditions, and assertions to be expressed in code using a uniform syntax, with options such as having different contract levels and custom violation handlers. The ISO Working Group, which is charged with the task of producing the draft standard, is very much in favour of contracts as a way of improving C++'s safety. The draft committee has had disagreements before, but this time it is more serious as Bjarne doesn't agree.  As Herb Sutter puts it: _"The new factor is that Bjarne thinks WG21’s consensus on C++26 contracts is too wrong to accept. He has serious technical reasons (especially safety concerns) that, with his unique judgment and experience, he believes are decisive. Bjarne is never shy about telling WG21 when he thinks we’re going wrong. This time, for the first time in 36 years, he thinks a feature decision is so wrong that the DIS shouldn’t be approved"_ This raises the question of who owns the language? We have seen examples of other governance systems throwing up disagreements between originator and governing committees before, but this is new for C++.  Bjarne is very clear: _“design is fundamentally flawed: adding new insecurities, leaving key facilities implementation defined, restricting violation handling, adding overheads, incomplete, needing additions and modifications, having semantics determined by compiler switches, and more”_ The working group voted 90% for the feature, but the national bodies seem to have been swayed by Bjarne's criticism and they voted the draft down.  Now what? I'm not completely sure as the Working Group has a consensus that contracts should be in C++26 - ??how can it change this decision? Its opinion is supposed to be technical and nothing technical has changed. Herb Sutter suggests that there are a few options - remove contracts and re-vote, move on to C++29, go back to earlier drafts or restructure things so that everyone has the same equal vote.  What effect does this have on the C++ programmer? The most obvious is the delay of the C++26 standard and the uncertainly about the future of contracts. Code created using draft flags may have to be withdrawn and useful, non-controversial, features are similarly blighted.  Personally, I'd rather see a fork of the language into Bjarne's C++ and another version for the rest of them, but I don't think that is going to be popular
