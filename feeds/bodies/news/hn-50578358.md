- Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster, and Matei Coiculescu, with heavy AI assistance, have written a human-readable proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Euler equations in three dimensions, which is formalized in Lean here.

- Levent Alpöge, using an internal AI model, has generated an (enormous) Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. This Lean Zulip thread is probably the best place to follow the efforts to verify the proof and extract the strategy of proof (which, from very initial analysis, appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry). Under GRH the bound follows at once from the formula, so it is equivalent to GRH for these characters, and the work is all in proving it. The bound is proved in the spirit of Chebyshev, by comparing divisibility with size. The untwisted sum is a count of divisibility, since ![\sum_{n \le y} \Lambda(n)(y/n - 1) = \sum_p \log p \sum_i \max(y/p^i - 1, 0)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Csum_%7Bn+%5Cle+y%7D+%5CLambda%28n%29%28y%2Fn+-+1%29+%3D+%5Csum_p+%5Clog+p+%5Csum_i+%5Cmax%28y%2Fp%5Ei+-+1%2C+0%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), much as ![\log n! = \sum_p \log p \sum_i \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+n%21+%3D+%5Csum_p+%5Clog+p+%5Csum_i+%5Clfloor+n%2Fp%5Ei+%5Crfloor&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). For ![n!](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n%21&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) itself the prime valuations account for the size exactly. Information comes from setting an independent lower bound for divisibility against an independent upper bound for size. In the proof the lower bound for divisibility is at the primes ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) inert in an imaginary quadratic field ![K](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=K&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) (with a correction at the ramified prime), at least about ![a/p + a/p^3 + \dots](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a%2Fp+%2B+a%2Fp%5E3+%2B+%5Cdots&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) powers of each, where ![a](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is a size parameter. Only odd powers appear because ![\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_K(n))](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CLambda%28n%29%281+-+%5Cchi_K%28n%29%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is ![2 \log p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=2+%5Clog+p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) on the odd powers of an inert ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and vanishes on its even powers and at split primes. A one-sided bound is what Landau’s theorem takes. Alpöge’s note calls the argument a descendant of Stepanov’s, with a chain of theta lifts, each “mimicking multiplicity”, standing in for the multiplicity that is hard to find over the integers. At the scales the proof uses, the integer’s role is played by maps between lattices over a number field containing an abelian CM field ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), one map for each word in a family. The declarations compare each map’s pairing with a Bessel-type pairing, up to isometries and a nonzero scalar.
