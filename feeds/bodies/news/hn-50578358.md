- Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster, and Matei Coiculescu, with heavy AI assistance, have written a human-readable proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Euler equations in three dimensions, which is formalized in Lean here.
- Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, again with heavy AI usage, have generated a proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Navier Stokes equations in three dimensions, again with a Lean formalization. Here, the paper is technically human-readable and the general strategy of proof is broadly clear (following one of the expected paths to this result, namely to numerically build an approximate discretely self-similar solution and then use rigorous perturbation theory to convert this to an exact solution); but more work needs to be done to create a proper, professionally written proof.
- Levent Alpöge, using an internal AI model, has generated an (enormous) Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. This Lean Zulip thread is probably the best place to follow the efforts to verify the proof and extract the strategy of proof (which, from very initial analysis, appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry). The untwisted sum is a count of divisibility, since ![\sum_{n \le y} \Lambda(n)(y/n - 1) = \sum_p \log p \sum_i \max(y/p^i - 1, 0)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Csum_%7Bn+%5Cle+y%7D+%5CLambda%28n%29%28y%2Fn+-+1%29+%3D+%5Csum_p+%5Clog+p+%5Csum_i+%5Cmax%28y%2Fp%5Ei+-+1%2C+0%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), much as ![\log n! = \sum_p \log p \sum_i \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+n%21+%3D+%5Csum_p+%5Clog+p+%5Csum_i+%5Clfloor+n%2Fp%5Ei+%5Crfloor&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). For ![n!](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n%21&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) itself the prime valuations account for the size exactly. Information comes from setting an independent lower bound for divisibility against an independent upper bound for size. In the proof the lower bound for divisibility is at the primes ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) inert in an imaginary quadratic field ![K](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=K&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) (with a correction at the ramified prime), at least about ![a/p + a/p^3 + \dots](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a%2Fp+%2B+a%2Fp%5E3+%2B+%5Cdots&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) powers of each, where ![a](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is a size parameter. Only odd powers appear because ![\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_K(n))](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CLambda%28n%29%281+-+%5Cchi_K%28n%29%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is ![2 \log p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=2+%5Clog+p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) on the odd powers of an inert ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and vanishes on its even powers and at split primes. So this count belongs to ![\zeta(s)/L(s, \chi_K)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Czeta%28s%29%2FL%28s%2C+%5Cchi_K%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) in the way the factorial’s belongs to ![\zeta(s)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Czeta%28s%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The size has to match this count to second order, down to the term linear in ![a](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and that is where the constant ![\log \Omega](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+%5COmega&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) below comes from. Chebyshev matched only ![a \log a](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a+%5Clog+a&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and got constants. What is then left of “divisibility ≤ size” is a one-sided bound on the centred ramp sums twisted by characters, as strong as the errors in the two estimates. A one-sided bound is what Landau’s theorem takes. Alpöge’s note calls the argument a descendant of Stepanov’s, with a chain of theta lifts, each “mimicking multiplicity”, standing in for the multiplicity that is hard to find over the integers. At the scales the proof uses, the integer’s role is played by maps between lattices over a number field containing an abelian CM field ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), one map for each word in a family. Each map is a composite of ![m = x^{1/2 - O(\varepsilon)}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=m+%3D+x%5E%7B1%2F2+-+O%28%5Cvarepsilon%29%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) kernels, attached to a chain of definite Hermitian spaces over ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). In Alpöge’s note these are theta lifts between totally definite unitary groups, and the entries of the maps are periods. The declarations compare each map’s pairing with a Bessel-type pairing, up to isometries and a nonzero scalar. I have not checked that this is the classical period. ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is the compositum of a real layer of the 2-power cyclotomic tower, of degree about ![x^{2+8\varepsilon}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x%5E%7B2%2B8%5Cvarepsilon%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), the field cut out by ![\psi](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cpsi&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and two imaginary quadratic fields ![\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%28%5Csqrt%7B-q%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=K+%3D+%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%28%5Csqrt%7B-d%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), for very large primes ![q](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=q&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![d](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=d&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) chosen below. Summed over the chain, the lifts have to gain at least about ![m\,(a/p + a/p^3 + \dots)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=m%5C%2C%28a%2Fp+%2B+a%2Fp%5E3+%2B+%5Cdots%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) powers of ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) at each inert ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) up to a cutoff, with explicit losses. Behind this is the local statement that a normalised theta series becomes ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-integral once you substitute the formal logarithm of a CM elliptic curve, whose formal group has height 2 at inert ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). An ![a](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-th derivative in height 2 gains about ![a/p + a/p^3 + \dots](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a%2Fp+%2B+a%2Fp%5E3+%2B+%5Cdots&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) powers of ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and I read the count as coming from there (no declaration says so). (Here ![a = x b](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a+%3D+x+b&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) with ![b = \exp(x^\varepsilon)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=b+%3D+%5Cexp%28x%5E%5Cvarepsilon%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The ramp sums are evaluated at ![y = a/w](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=y+%3D+a%2Fw&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) for weights ![w](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=w&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) attached to the embeddings of ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and the relevant ![w](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=w&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) are near ![b](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=b&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), so ![y](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=y&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is near ![x](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5).) Divisibility and size are compared through a slope inequality of Bost’s type, applied to all the maps at once, and the main term ![a \log a - a - a \log \Omega](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a+%5Clog+a+-+a+-+a+%5Clog+%5COmega&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) per lift comes from a separate covolume estimate, where ![\log \Omega = \gamma + \log(2\pi/\mathrm{rd}_E) - \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi_K)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+%5COmega+%3D+%5Cgamma+%2B+%5Clog%282%5Cpi%2F%5Cmathrm%7Brd%7D_E%29+-+%5Cfrac%7BL%27%7D%7BL%7D%281%2C+%5Cchi_K%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![\mathrm{rd}_E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7Brd%7D_E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is the root discriminant of ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). Subtracting the main term on the size side from the prime-power count on the divisibility side gives, exactly, a combination of the ![G(\theta, \cdot)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=G%28%5Ctheta%2C+%5Ccdot%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) over the characters ![\theta](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Ctheta&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) of ![\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7BGal%7D%28E%2F%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), read as Dirichlet characters (orthogonality of characters). After averaging along the tower only the powers of ![\psi](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cpsi&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) survive, alone or times ![\chi_{-q}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cchi_%7B-q%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), ![\chi_{-d}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cchi_%7B-d%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) or ![\chi_{-q}\chi_{-d}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cchi_%7B-q%7D%5Cchi_%7B-d%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The untwisted powers are the sum we want. I don’t know a pointwise bound for the twisted ones short of GRH, and the archive doesn’t use one. It picks ![q](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=q&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![d](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=d&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), by a mean-square estimate over primes and a count of zeros, so that the twisted ramp sums are ![O(y^{1/2+\varepsilon})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=O%28y%5E%7B1%2F2%2B%5Cvarepsilon%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) on a window. I think that is one reason the result comes on windows and not for every large ![x](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The total loss is ![x^{O(\varepsilon)}(x/m + m + \sqrt{x})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x%5E%7BO%28%5Cvarepsilon%29%7D%28x%2Fm+%2B+m+%2B+%5Csqrt%7Bx%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), hence ![m](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=m&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) near ![\sqrt{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Csqrt%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The integrality comes with an error term, and its arithmetic part involves Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists. Take the classes in $latex H^1(E, \mathbb{Q}*\ell/\mathbb{Z}*\ell(-e))$ that are unramified outside ![\ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and trivial at the places above ![\ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and sum the lengths of these groups over primes ![\ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and twists ![e \ge 1](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=e+%5Cge+1&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) below ![x^{401}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x%5E%7B401%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) with weight ![\log \ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The archive bounds the minus part by valuations of generalized Bernoulli numbers, as one does classically, away from 2 and the primes dividing ![[E:\mathbb{Q}\]](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5BE%3A%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%5D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The plus part is what the note calls a “weakening of Vandiver”. In its main case it is proved from three-dimensional Galois representations attached to automorphic forms on unitary groups, a vanishing of Selmer groups of Kato’s type, and congruences between the Frobenius traces of those representations and the reducible trace ![1 + \Psi + \Psi^{-1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=1+%2B+%5CPsi+%2B+%5CPsi%5E%7B-1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), where ![\Psi](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CPsi&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is the twisted character. The main estimate there is polynomial in the conductor and the twist, but the assembled bound picks up one more term of size ![\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cexp%28x%5E%7B4%5Cvarepsilon%2F5%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) from finitely many small primes. That’s still below the ![\exp(x^\varepsilon/2)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cexp%28x%5E%5Cvarepsilon%2F2%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) the integrality can afford. There is one special case I can compare with the literature. Over ![\mathbb{Q}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) the archive’s bound gives the vanishing of the eigenspace of the ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-part of the class group of ![\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%28%5Czeta_p%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) for the ![(p - n)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%28p+-+n%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-th power of the Teichmüller character, ![n \ge 3](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n+%5Cge+3&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) odd, as soon as ![\log p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) exceeds ![C n^A](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=C+n%5EA&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), with constants ![C](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=C&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![A](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=A&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) that are not made explicit. Toward Vandiver’s conjecture, Soulé (1999) proves this, for ![n \ge 5](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n+%5Cge+5&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), when ![\log p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) exceeds ![n^{224 n^4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n%5E%7B224+n%5E4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). On the Langlands side, what I see of local Langlands on the main route is the existence of a map with a short list of properties (class field theory on characters, Zelevinsky’s segments, depth zero over ![\mathbb{Q}_p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D_p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)). The map isn’t asked to preserve epsilon factors of pairs. But a local converse theorem for ![\mathrm{GL}_n](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7BGL%7D_n&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), which does involve them, is among the inputs passed to the Galois side, and I haven’t checked whether it is actually used there. The Galois representations attached to regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representations of ![\mathrm{GL}_n](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7BGL%7D_n&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) over CM fields are used in full for their existence, with only the local–global compatibility weakened.
