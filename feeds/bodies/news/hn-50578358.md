- Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster, and Matei Coiculescu, with heavy AI assistance, have written a human-readable proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Euler equations in three dimensions, which is formalized in Lean here.
- Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, again with heavy AI usage, have generated a proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Navier Stokes equations in three dimensions, again with a Lean formalization. Here, the paper is technically human-readable and the general strategy of proof is broadly clear (following one of the expected paths to this result, namely to numerically build an approximate discretely self-similar solution and then use rigorous perturbation theory to convert this to an exact solution); but more work needs to be done to create a proper, professionally written proof.
- Levent Alpöge, using an internal AI model, has generated an (enormous) Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. This Lean Zulip thread is probably the best place to follow the efforts to verify the proof and extract the strategy of proof (which, from very initial analysis, appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry). I imagine the digestion process will take some time, though in this case at least we have a human author to communicate with to assist with this task.

Behind this is the local statement that a normalised theta series becomes ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-integral once you substitute the formal logarithm of a CM elliptic curve, whose formal group has height 2 at inert ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). An ![a](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-th derivative in height 2 gains about ![a/p + a/p^3 + \dots](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a%2Fp+%2B+a%2Fp%5E3+%2B+%5Cdots&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) powers of ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and I read the count as coming from there (no declaration says so). (Here ![a = x b](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a+%3D+x+b&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) with ![b = \exp(x^\varepsilon)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=b+%3D+%5Cexp%28x%5E%5Cvarepsilon%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The ramp sums are evaluated at ![y = a/w](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=y+%3D+a%2Fw&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) for weights ![w](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=w&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) attached to the embeddings of ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and the relevant ![w](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=w&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) are near ![b](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=b&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), so ![y](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=y&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is near ![x](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5).) Divisibility and size are compared through a slope inequality of Bost’s type, applied to all the maps at once, and the main term ![a \log a - a - a \log \Omega](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=a+%5Clog+a+-+a+-+a+%5Clog+%5COmega&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) per lift comes from a separate covolume estimate, where ![\log \Omega = \gamma + \log(2\pi/\mathrm{rd}_E) - \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi_K)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+%5COmega+%3D+%5Cgamma+%2B+%5Clog%282%5Cpi%2F%5Cmathrm%7Brd%7D_E%29+-+%5Cfrac%7BL%27%7D%7BL%7D%281%2C+%5Cchi_K%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![\mathrm{rd}_E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7Brd%7D_E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is the root discriminant of ![E](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=E&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). Subtracting the main term on the size side from the prime-power count on the divisibility side gives, exactly, a combination of the ![G(\theta, \cdot)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=G%28%5Ctheta%2C+%5Ccdot%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) over the characters ![\theta](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Ctheta&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) of ![\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7BGal%7D%28E%2F%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), read as Dirichlet characters (orthogonality of characters). After averaging along the tower only the powers of ![\psi](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cpsi&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) survive, alone or times ![\chi_{-q}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cchi_%7B-q%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), ![\chi_{-d}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cchi_%7B-d%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) or ![\chi_{-q}\chi_{-d}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cchi_%7B-q%7D%5Cchi_%7B-d%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The untwisted powers are the sum we want. I don’t know a pointwise bound for the twisted ones short of GRH, and the archive doesn’t use one. It picks ![q](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=q&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![d](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=d&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), by a mean-square estimate over primes and a count of zeros, so that the twisted ramp sums are ![O(y^{1/2+\varepsilon})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=O%28y%5E%7B1%2F2%2B%5Cvarepsilon%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) on a window. I think that is one reason the result comes on windows and not for every large ![x](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The total loss is ![x^{O(\varepsilon)}(x/m + m + \sqrt{x})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x%5E%7BO%28%5Cvarepsilon%29%7D%28x%2Fm+%2B+m+%2B+%5Csqrt%7Bx%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), hence ![m](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=m&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) near ![\sqrt{x}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Csqrt%7Bx%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The integrality comes with an error term, and its arithmetic part involves Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists. Take the classes in $latex H^1(E, \mathbb{Q}*\ell/\mathbb{Z}*\ell(-e))$ that are unramified outside ![\ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and trivial at the places above ![\ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and sum the lengths of these groups over primes ![\ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and twists ![e \ge 1](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=e+%5Cge+1&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) below ![x^{401}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=x%5E%7B401%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) with weight ![\log \ell](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+%5Cell&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The archive bounds the minus part by valuations of generalized Bernoulli numbers, as one does classically, away from 2 and the primes dividing ![[E:\mathbb{Q}\]](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5BE%3A%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%5D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). The plus part is what the note calls a “weakening of Vandiver”. In its main case it is proved from three-dimensional Galois representations attached to automorphic forms on unitary groups, a vanishing of Selmer groups of Kato’s type, and congruences between the Frobenius traces of those representations and the reducible trace ![1 + \Psi + \Psi^{-1}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=1+%2B+%5CPsi+%2B+%5CPsi%5E%7B-1%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), where ![\Psi](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5CPsi&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) is the twisted character. The main estimate there is polynomial in the conductor and the twist, but the assembled bound picks up one more term of size ![\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cexp%28x%5E%7B4%5Cvarepsilon%2F5%7D%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) from finitely many small primes. That’s still below the ![\exp(x^\varepsilon/2)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cexp%28x%5E%5Cvarepsilon%2F2%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) the integrality can afford. There is one special case I can compare with the literature. Over ![\mathbb{Q}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) the archive’s bound gives the vanishing of the eigenspace of the ![p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-part of the class group of ![\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D%28%5Czeta_p%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) for the ![(p - n)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%28p+-+n%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)-th power of the Teichmüller character, ![n \ge 3](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n+%5Cge+3&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) odd, as soon as ![\log p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) exceeds ![C n^A](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=C+n%5EA&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), with constants ![C](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=C&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![A](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=A&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) that are not made explicit. Toward Vandiver’s conjecture, Soulé (1999) proves this, for ![n \ge 5](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n+%5Cge+5&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), when ![\log p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Clog+p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) exceeds ![n^{224 n^4}](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=n%5E%7B224+n%5E4%7D&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5). On the Langlands side, what I see of local Langlands on the main route is the existence of a map with a short list of properties (class field theory on characters, Zelevinsky’s segments, depth zero over ![\mathbb{Q}_p](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathbb%7BQ%7D_p&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5)). The map isn’t asked to preserve epsilon factors of pairs. But a local converse theorem for ![\mathrm{GL}_n](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7BGL%7D_n&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), which does involve them, is among the inputs passed to the Galois side, and I haven’t checked whether it is actually used there. The Galois representations attached to regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representations of ![\mathrm{GL}_n](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%5Cmathrm%7BGL%7D_n&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) over CM fields are used in full for their existence, with only the local–global compatibility weakened. Among the statements I could not find in the literature are the central inequality itself, the approximate orthogonality between the maps for different words (a bound on the off-diagonal blocks of their Gram matrix), a lower bound for the average over components of normalised arithmetic self-intersection numbers in towers of unitary Shimura varieties of signature ![(r, s)](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=%28r%2C+s%29&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) with ![s \ge 2](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=s+%5Cge+2&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5) and ![r \ge 2s + 1](https://s0.wp.com/latex.php?latex=r+%5Cge+2s+%2B+1&bg=ffffff&fg=000&s=0&c=20201002&zoom=4.5), and the bound for the plus part summed over primes and twists. What I’ve checked independently is the analytic end. The window form of Landau’s theorem and the equivalence with GRH were proved again from scratch, and the identity was re-proved from its statement and checked numerically on small fields. For the middle I have only read statements of declarations, and “theta lift” and “period” are Alpöge’s words and the names in the code. I haven’t checked that the objects are built the way those names suggest, or that the middle constructs what the end uses.
