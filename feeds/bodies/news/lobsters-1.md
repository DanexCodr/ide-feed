On 17th October, eight eMudhra intermediate CAs chaining to emSign ECC Root CA - G3 were found to have [trivially recoverable private keys](https://emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/). They were revoked the next day, and Mozilla and Chrome are now distrusting the G3 root. Separately, on 19th October eMudhra revoked 9,356 certificates on its unaffected RSA hierarchy (emSign Root CA - G1) with the reason “Superseded”. That batch matches every unexpired certificate missing a certificate-type policy OID required by emSign’s own CP/CPS ([Bug 2082782](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2082782)).

On 21st October, MeitY [posted this thread](https://x.com/GoI_MeitY/status/2112914722656076060):

> A security incident concerning eMudhra’s Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) was reported in the public domain. […]
>
> However, some social media accounts are indulging in fear mongering about the impact of this incident. […]
>
> **The impact of this incident on Indian websites and applications have been very limited and for a very short interval.**
>
> — [@GoI_MeitY](https://x.com/GoI_MeitY/status/2112914722656076060), 21st October 2026, 16:31 CEST

That last claim is wrong on both counts. The interval wasn’t short: the flawed keys were generated in April 2025, so anyone could have recovered them for 18 months before the report. The impact isn’t limited either: the compromised intermediates barely issued anything, but Mozilla and Chrome are now distrusting the entire G3 root, so eMudhra [estimates 30,000 certificates](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2083449#c19) from its unaffected G3 intermediates must be migrated. From what I could see, most of those seem to belong to US universities using eMudhra through InCommon rather than Indian sites.

Meanwhile, the 19th October revocations have left **Indian government sites serving revoked certificates days later**, including four bodies under MeitY itself: [STPI](https://www.stpi.in/), [Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/), [UIDAI](https://lea.uidai.gov.in/), and the Controller of Certifying Authorities, whose [India PKI site](https://indiapki.cca.gov.in/) is serving a revoked eMudhra certificate. Notably, Controller of Certifying Authorities regulates eMudhra’s CA business in India.

I checked the 8,947 hosts in the [IGOD directory](https://github.com/rishirishhh/IGOD-Dataset) of Indian government websites. As of 23rd October, 156 of them are still serving one of these revoked certificates. All of them were revoked between 15:34 and 15:49 CEST on 19th October, and are listed in eMudhra’s CRLs for [DV/G2A-1](http://crl.emsign.com?EMDVTLSCAG2A1.crl), [OV/G2A-1](http://crl.emsign.com?EMOVTLSCAG2A1.crl), [EV/G2A-1](http://crl.emsign.com?EMEVTLSCAG2A1.crl) and [G1](http://crl.emsign.com?emSignSSLCAG1.crl).

Whether these sites open depends on your browser. Firefox ships [CRLite](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2020/01/09/crlite-part-1-all-web-pki-revocations-compressed/), which covers every revocation, and refuses to load them. Chrome’s [CRLSet](https://www.chromium.org/Home/chromium-security/crlsets/) (version 10856, 24th October) has none of these certificates, nor any of the 6,235 entries on the DV/G2A-1 CRL, so Chrome, Edge and Android load them fine. So does Safari on iOS, which only checks OCSP for EV certificates. Two sites on the list have one: [www.isea.gov.in](https://www.isea.gov.in/) and [nfscnagpur.nic.in](https://nfscnagpur.nic.in/), and emSign’s OCSP responder reports the isea.gov.in certificate as revoked, so Safari on iOS refuses to load it. [SSL Labs](https://www.ssllabs.com/ssltest/analyze.html?d=aaib.gov.in&latest) checks the CRL itself and marks all of these as Grade F / revoked.

Revocations happen (GoDaddy just revoked 4 lakh certificates), but they are usually accompanied by notifications to subscribers to 
get them to new valid certificates. I’m not sure if eMudhra is doing any of the sort, now that they don’t care about their TLS business.
