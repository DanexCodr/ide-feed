**Shiny object syndrome** is the situation where people focus undue attention on an idea that is new and [trendy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fad), yet drop it in its entirety as soon as something new can take its place.

Shiny object syndrome (SOS) is a [pop-cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_culture), psychological concept where people focus on a new and fashionable idea, regardless of how valuable or helpful it may ultimately be.<sup>[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-1)</sup><sup>[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-2)</sup> While at the moment it seems to be something worth focusing one's attention upon, it is ultimately a [distraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distraction),<sup>[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-ent-3)</sup> either a personal distraction or something that is done intentionally to distract others.<sup>[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-4)</sup> People who face a [fear of missing out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_of_missing_out) are especially susceptible, as the distraction of shiny objects in themselves clouds judgment and focus.<sup>[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-5)</sup>

The term shiny object syndrome is often used when people mistake something small and focused and fixate on it to the extent that they lose the big picture.<sup>[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-6)</sup> This is used within management literature, popular [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) literature, and across the social and computer sciences.<sup>[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-7)</sup><sup>[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiny_object_syndrome#cite_note-8)</sup>
