## About the data

- Public power is common in cities and near federal dams, where power is cheap, and public utilities pay no income tax and borrow tax-free. That explains some of the gap, not why it grew.
- Most of the gap opened from 2020 to 2023 , during the energy price spike: 25% at investor-owned utilities, 11% at public utilities and co-ops. Before and since, both rose at about the same pace.
- After inflation, investor-owned prices rose 7.9% . Public and co-op prices fell 4.3% .
- Co-op homes have the highest bills, $159 a month in 2025 , because they use the most power.
- Homes that buy power from a retail supplier aren't in the investor-owned figures. Counting them, prices on investor-owned lines rose 36% .
- Price is what homes paid divided by the kilowatt-hours they used, from EIA's yearly survey of utilities. Federal utilities and the smallest ones are left out.
- The data shows what homes paid, not why. Investor-owned utilities earn a return for shareholders on what they build. Public utilities and co-ops have no shareholders. See the rate cases for increases utilities are asking for now.
