The Stack Overflow that as developers we used to rely on for the answers to our day-to-day queries was among the first casualties of generative AI. It is, however, continuing to function, even though its previous role as the prime resource for answers has been usurped by AI chatbots and its annual survey has things to tell us about the current developer landscape. Whereas at its zenith in 2018 the Stack Overflow Developer Survey saw the participation of 101,592 professionals and students across 183 countries, this year it attracted a much reduced response from 30,903 technologists from 169 countries. Even with a smaller sample size, the 2026 findings highlight several clear trends in the tools developers rely on:

  * **JavaScript holds the top spot:** Used by 62.0% of respondents, JavaScript remains the most widely used programming language for the 12th consecutive year, ahead of SQL (58.4%), HTML/CSS (58.2%), and Python (58.0%).
  * **Python leads for AI, Rust leads for loyalty:** While Python is the most used language specifically for AI development (39.0%) and the most "desired" language among developers who want to start using it (33.4%), Rust remains the most "admired" language, with 67.4% of current Rust developers saying they want to keep working with it.
  * **VS Code fends off AI-native challengers:** Despite the rise of AI-first editors like Cursor (14.9%) and Windsurf (3.2%), Visual Studio Code remains the dominant IDE at 70.9% usage.

As already noted, AI has fundamentally impacted Stack Overflow's role as a resource for answers and its traffic has dwindled. 64% visit less for simple questions, 29% ask fewer questions, and 27% contribute less. Only 1% contribute more. However, 16% now visit more specifically for verification of AI-supplied answers and source attribution, meaning SO is shifting from place to ask to a place to verify. A smaller segment (6%) also reports visiting more because AI-generated answers lead them back to the site. That need to double-check reflects a wider skepticism: while 62% view AI tools favorably, only 7% trust AI output for important work decisions, and 48% say they only trust it when they can easily verify the result. In years gone by, the Stack Overflow Survey has generally found that respondents were happy in their work roles. Not so the 2026 survey which reveals indicates growing dissatisfaction: Overall, developers are also noticeably less satisfied with their lot than they used to be. Only 22% of respondents describe themselves as "happy at work", down from 24% in 2025. Nearly a third (33%) are actively "not happy at work", up from 29% the previous year, while the largest share, 45%,report feeling merely "complacent" in their current roles.
