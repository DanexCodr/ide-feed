Is there a programmer on the planet who doesn't love regular expressions? OK, perhaps many of us just pretend and are really in awe and fear... What could be easier then viewing your favorite expression as a marble machine. This is a fun, instructive and impressive program written in JavaScript, well TypeScript to be more precise, with rendering by Three.js and help from React and React Three Fiber. The idea is simple - take a regular expression and construct a marble run with various types of tests and gates so that as each marble from a test string rolls through the machine it either ends up as a match or no-match: _"Regex Marble Run visualizes regular expression matching as a mechanical marble run. Marbles ride down birch-plywood tracks, test characters at brass gate arches, choose branches at enameled diverters, cycle through loop lifts, frame submatches in capture brackets, and trigger position sensors."_ You can tell from this discription that this is no limited implementation you can tryout backtracking and captures.  I said a few sentences ago that "The idea is simple" and it is but now consider the problem of translating a regular expression into such a marble run! Its a difficult problem that has much in common with any translation or compiling problem but with the added fun of organizing a 3D structure. This is why I said that the program was impressive. It is the work of Qcumbeer, aka Szymon Piecuch. The code is available on GitHub with an MIT licence, complete with a description of how to run it on Node.js, but if you just want to try it out there is a[ website](<https://www.regexmarblerun.com/>) with a live demo. You can enter your own regular expressions and test strings and watch as the marbles fall to the left or the right. Do notice that it really is 3D and you can use the mouse to view from any position: Does it help understand regular expressions? It didn't help me much in that respect, but I still enjoyed playing with it and watching it. I guess I'm just not a visual sort of reasoner - I prefer symbols. You might get more educational benefit from it but, whatever, it's a pleasure to watch.
