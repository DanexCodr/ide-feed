In November's edition of the TIOBE Index, Rust has dropped out of Top 10, losing its place to Go. At the same time, another Google-originated language, Dart, has entered the top 20 ousting Julia after only one month. At the top of the table, Java is back in the top 3, after over three years in 4th position. As regular readers of I-Programmer will already know, we have an enduring interest in the TIOBE Programming Community Index, which monitors the ups and downs of program language popularity, where popularity is based on a combination of factors. The ratings take into account the number of skilled engineers world-wide, the availability of courses and third-party vendors using data from Google, Amazon, Wikipedia, Bing and more than 20 other websites. As pointed out in [Languages That Stand The Test Of Time](<https://www.i-programmer.info/history/57-computer-languages/17888-languages-that-stand-the-test-of-time.html>), the TIOBE Index has a natural tendency to keeping the status quo which means that long-lived, well-established languages are to be found at the top of the index and the entry of a new language into the index is a rare event. For example, we reported on Zig, a language being developed as a replacement for C entering the TIOBE Top 50 in 2023. Zig is now somewhere in the lower 50 of the 100 languages that are included, while C remains in 2nd position which has been the position it has held for most of the 25 years covered by the Top 10 Chart, overtaking Java from time to time to take the top position. If you think of the ratings as shares of a pie divided into 100 pieces, there is one huge slice— Python, accounting for a over a fifth of the pie, then comes C with a slice slightly less than half of Python's. Java and C++ then have smaller slices you could just and C#'s share is around half of their average size. Over half the pie has now been shared between just five languages. The rest of the Pie is divided into four slices, each slightly larger than the previous one for the langages in bottom half of the Top 10; the bottom half of the Top 20; those in positions 21 thru 50; and the remaining 50 languages that are listed alphabetically rather than ranked and whose average share is 0.13% barely a crumb. 

  * **Python:** 20.86%
  * **C:** 11.48%
  * **Java:** 8.89%
  * **C++:** 8.35%
  * **C#:** 3.39%
  * **Next 5 (#6–10):** 10.17% _(JavaScript, R, SQL, Visual Basic, Go)_
  * **Next 10 (#11–20):** 11.12% _(Rust, PHP, Fortran, Scratch, Perl, MATLAB, Assembly, Swift, Delphi, Dart)_
  * **Next 30 (#21–50):** 12.81%
  * **Final 50 (#51–100):** **12.93%**

After that digression, let's return to this month's movements. Java has overtaken C++, but as you can see from this month's Top 10 chart they are in lock step and the difference between them is insignificant. More obvious is that Python is way ahead of all the competition and that C has an edge over Java and C++, for more discussion of this see [TIOBE - Two To Rule Them All](<https://www.i-programmer.info/news/98-languages/18178-tiobe-two-to-rule-them-all.html>) in which Mike James argued that this: _confirms the standard model of programming - code is written in Python and C and everything else is just noise._ Back in July we reported [Rust Enters TIOBE Top 10](<https://www.i-programmer.info/news/98-languages/19006-rust-enters-tiobe-top-10.html>). After four months in #10 Rust is now back at #11, which is still three places above its position, 14, a year ago. Given the way these rankings operate it only takes a very small month-on-month change for a language to lose its position and even, as has happened here it can gain in terms of its percentage ranking while still moving down. So Rust with 1.63% in November is now in #11 when it had been in #10 with 1.44%. Go, which is the language that now occupies #10, is however up on last month by 0.3%. Go is no stranger to the Top 10. It made Its first appearance there in March 2023, only to be ousted the following month. In February 2024 when it re-entered for the third time. It was at `#8`and reached an all-time high of #7 for a few months in Spring 2025. Having dropped out shortly afterwards it now enters the Top 10 for the fourth time. In terms of its prevalance, and the key role it occupies in cloud computing I would expect it to stay in the Top 10 and to climb off the bottom rung. But for that to happen some other language would have to lose share. The language that features in TIOBE's the November headline is Dart, which has moved into #20 from being at #26 a year ago.  Dart's, which originated at Google in 2011 as a "structured web programming language", saw its highest ever position in the TIOBE index in January 2017 when it reached #17. At that time it had seen a meteoric rise and was  _the fastest growing programming language at Google in 2016 with millions of lines of code written [in it]_ In 2018 Dart saw an even more dramatic decline and having left the top 20 bounced around in relative obscurity.  Commenting on Dart's surprise entry into the Top 20, Paul Jansen, TIOBE Software's CEO who maintains the index, notes: _When talking about Dart, you cannot ignore Flutter, Google's framework for building cross-platform mobile applications. Flutter is the main driving force behind Dart's growing popularity. In fact, Flutter has become one of the major technologies for cross-platform mobile app development._ This is a timely reminder that to be a success a programming language needs to have a strong ecosytem and also a recognisable role
