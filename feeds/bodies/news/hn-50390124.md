What it does not verify is the integrity of the calling application and the runtime operating system. In payment systems, compromised or tampered execution environments present a major attack vector. EPI Central Services and our underlying payment tokenisation components require assurance on several layers before issuing tokens with payment authority: that the application binary has not been tampered with or repackaged, that the app is distributed through an authentic channel, and that the operating system has not been modified or hooked by debugging or emulation tools. Google Play Integrity provides these combined signals in a signed token that our backend verifies server-to-server. Hardware key attestation is an important building block, but on its own it does not give the full application and OS integrity verification that our risk and fraud models require. Sidenote: apparently Wero does not require payments to be made through a mobile app. There's a separate web-to-web flow (similar to current iDeal payments that are completed on laptops/desktops), but ASN chose not to implement this.
