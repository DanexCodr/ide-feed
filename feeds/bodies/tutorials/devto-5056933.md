## The Local Results Remained

The air conditioner in the test room was blowing toward the door. Lena moved her chair half a step out of the draft, where she could still see Leo's screen. An ACL engineer checked service status at the other end. The client's designated witness confirmed the procedure sheet before the test began.

The frozen synthetic dataset covered three simulated sites. Task, methodology, component, configuration, and dataset digest were the same in both runs. Run IDs differed. Caches and pending queues had been checked empty in clean instances. The 120-second observation window, retry policy, and end conditions had been approved on the procedure sheet in advance. They were the same for both runs.

In the first run, the shared test service was reachable.

Local results appeared at the three simulated sites in sequence. Finally, the group package formed too.

Leo did not switch windows. He opened the input associations, checked the identifiers and received results one by one, and saved the event records. Lena marked the first run as valid on her checklist.

For the second run, only the approved shared test service was isolated. Identity services and other necessary connections remained available. That one change distinguished the isolation rules from the original rules.

Local results appeared again. Leo kept the aggregation-record window open. He did not rerun the test.

The 120-second window ended. The group package was incomplete. He saved the record for that interval.

The ACL engineer brought up the technical appendix.

“That's the dependency we declared. Local processing still works without the shared service. The group package has to wait for aggregation.”

“Right.” Leo put the two original-record references side by side. “Now we can distinguish them.”

He saved the two sets of raw event files in the project's A and B directories. In the terminal, he generated a checksum list and checked it:

```bash
shasum -a 256 A/events.json B/events.json > originals.sha256
shasum -a 256 -c originals.sha256
```

```text
A/events.json: OK
B/events.json: OK
```

Lena opened the list. Each of the two digests pointed to its file.

“File fingerprints,” Leo said. “**The list can be changed too. It doesn't prove that the events in the files happened.**”

Keeping the original-record references, he organized the run conditions, statuses, and evidence locations in `runs.json`, then ran the comparison script:

```python
import json
from pathlib import Path

runs = json.loads(Path("runs.json").read_text())
keys = ("task", "method", "component", "config", "dataset_sha256", "window_s")
if len(runs) != 2 or any(k not in r or r[k] is None for r in runs for k in keys):
    raise ValueError("missing comparison conditions")
if any(not isinstance(r[k], str) or not r[k].strip() for r in runs for k in keys[:-1]):
    raise ValueError("invalid comparison identifiers")
if any(type(r["window_s"]) not in (int, float) or not 0 < r["window_s"] < float("inf") for r in runs):
    raise ValueError("invalid observation window")
if any(runs[0][k] != runs[1][k] for k in keys):
    raise ValueError("conditions differ")
if [r.get("shared_service") for r in runs] != ["reachable", "isolated"]:
    raise ValueError("wrong control pair")
for r in runs:
    if not r.get("run_id") or not r.get("evidence"):
        raise ValueError("missing run identity or evidence reference")
if runs[0]["run_id"] == runs[1]["run_id"]:
    raise ValueError("duplicate run identity")
for r in runs:
    print(r["run_id"], "window_s=" + str(r["window_s"]))
    for key in ("local_result", "group_package"):
        print(" ", key + ":", r.get(key) or "UNKNOWN")
    print("  evidence:", r["evidence"])
```

```text
A window_s=120
  local_result: READY
  group_package: COMPLETE
  evidence: A/events.json
B window_s=120
  local_result: READY
  group_package: INCOMPLETE_IN_WINDOW
  evidence: B/events.json
```

Leo moved the cursor to the second run's final entry. The client witness confirmed that the original connection rules had been restored. The test sessions ended as planned.

He pointed to the window's end time in the record.

“**One hundred and twenty seconds. That's where we stop.**”

Lena opened the original-record references and checked the local results against the group package entries. The files were labeled synthetic. They had not used the overseas subsidiary's actual materials.

“What about the mapping the group report needs?” she asked.

“**This doesn't calculate it. It only compares the two synthetic runs.**” Leo closed the output and returned to the raw records.

The ACL engineer confirmed that the actual results would include applicability and inclusion-and-exclusion notes. The tested interfaces could be reused. The synthetic package did not replace those supporting materials.

## The Third Cup: Their Own Deliverables

The umbrella stand by the door was half full of water. Leo sat at a small table against the wall. He pushed his laptop farther in to make room for her. Lena hung up her coat and had barely sat down when the man placed a Long Black beside her and a Flat White in front of Leo, the handles clear of the laptops.

He cleared the little dish at the table's edge and returned the damp cloth to his tray. The door closed. Tires passing through rainwater sounded quieter beyond the glass.

“Raining again?” Leo glanced at the drops on her coat.

“Started just as I crossed the street.” Lena took a napkin to dry her hands. “You missed it?”

“Got here ten minutes earlier.”

The man shifted the umbrella stand inward to let another customer through. Lena put away the napkin and opened the report page she was permitted to take out. It showed only the project code and the current scope, not the client environment's name. Leo angled his engineering statement toward her.

“Write up the local part and deliver it,” she said. “Leave the group work for the later stage. The client doesn't have to throw away what we've done.”

“Agreed. The integration can stay too. They won't have to reconnect everything if they change aggregation suppliers.” Leo opened the milestones. His cursor moved from the local item to the group item. “But this can't stay under completed.”

Lena showed him the proposed title. He read “Group Assessment Solution Validation,” scrolled down a page, then returned to it.

“The scope is on page two,” she said.

“What if someone screenshots page one? Is this for the implementation example or the client?”

“Separate uses. The client already asked whether a narrower solution could keep this round open.”

“Then list what the narrower solution is worth.”

Lena enlarged the retained option in his engineering statement. “**Which budget decision does it help the client make?** If all they keep is an interface, how do they choose next year's suppliers?”

Leo lifted his coffee but did not drink. A customer by the window stood, chair legs scraping the floor. He waited for the customer to pass the table before setting down his cup.

“It can't answer that in full. It can reduce future integration costs. To continue the group comparison, they need to retain engineering support and your people.”

He slid the title back toward her. “**If your report still reads as though the group work is complete, who takes responsibility for that conclusion?**”

Lena changed the report title to “Validation of Local Processing and Synthetic Aggregation Conditions.” On the next line, she added the unfinished group work and deleted the broad wording from the proposed example citation.

Leo revised the engineering milestones. Local integration became a separate item. Reconciliation of the group results stayed in the later stage. The conditions attached to the expected later payments changed with it. He did not move them into the completed column.

Both coffees cooled. Lena checked the revision and saved her company's file. Leo saved his own.

The man came over, cleared the neighboring table, and waited for Lena to move her laptop a little farther in before putting down a clean water glass. He held the tray upright beside his leg and withdrew through the narrow gap. **Both screens were still lit, each holding a different deliverable.**

“This is the version I'll send the client,” Lena said.

“I'll send ours.” Leo finished his coffee and set the cup on its saucer. She checked her attachments and finished hers. The man took both cups without touching the laptops.

## Submission, Approval, Decision

On the sixth working day, the two companies submitted their revised files separately. Lena checked the synthetic results, overseas use application, and unfinished group work in the submission package, then saved the acknowledgment.

On the eighth, the overseas subsidiary's approval arrived. Recipient, materials, AI processing, and retention conditions matched the application. Public disclosure was not authorized.

ACL updated the entry. Lena checked the approval. Leo replied, “Permission resolved. Continue the checks.” ACL then submitted the overseas subsidiary's actual results and source notes within the approved scope. The materials entered the project channel.

Before the budget meeting on the tenth working day, ACL sent the group comparison working draft assembled by its system. Lena opened the file and checked the status beneath the header:

```text
Group comparison | working draft
Overseas use for this project: approved; approval attached
Actual results: received; report versions, periods, and control-item sources retained
Overseas results: scope reconciliation incomplete, including inclusions and exclusions
```

She opened a control item's source. A single requirement in one report had been split into several items in another. The mapping retained each report's definitions. Where no reliable correspondence had been established, the status remained unresolved. Notes beside the local exclusions asked whether they could carry over to the group scope. She turned to the last page. No group score had been calculated by averaging the three percentages.

Leo marked the inputs and configurations still needing reconciliation. Lena circled the corresponding entries on VeriTest's review sheet and entered the staff who would have to remain assigned.

She sent the latest draft and its limitations to the client. Procurement accepted the update even though the day-six package had already been submitted.

The budget meeting took place in the room where the project had started. The group's governance budget owner opened the subsidiary's approval, then switched to the working draft.

“Approved, and delivered,” he said. “**What do we get if we keep these people assigned?**”

The ACL representative brought forward the continuation proposal. “We reuse the tested integration and existing evidence. We don't start over. Finalizing the remaining scope gives you the group comparison needed to assess whether consolidation is worthwhile. You can reserve resources conditionally, with activation still dependent on complete supporting evidence.”

Leo projected CoreStack's allocation. “We need engineering support to reconcile the overseas results with the inputs, configuration, and inclusion scope. The integration we've already completed doesn't have to be done again.”

Lena opened the review-staff allocation. “Our work is methodology applicability and the final basis for comparison. The working draft hasn't completed that review.”

Procurement checked the extension clauses. Each explained the resources their company would need to retain.

“And if we don't reserve them now?”

“We settle the preparation work and release the later staffing slots,” Lena said. “There won't be a group comparison in this round. You can initiate another project later. But we can't keep these people assigned here at no cost while we wait.”

The client placed next cycle's existing service arrangements beside the working draft. All three areas could continue as they were.

“Can you finish this without additional support?” the budget owner asked.

The ACL representative checked the resource page. “No. **We can reduce rework. We can't make the remaining work cost nothing.**”

Lena scrolled to the review-staff allocation. It stopped in the center of the screen. Leo's engineering allocation remained visible beside it.

“**Then no reservation for this round**,” the budget owner said. “We can see the path. But the evidence we have now doesn't justify keeping these resources committed to it. Keep the existing arrangements.”

He switched back to next cycle's existing services.

ACL asked for the continuation option and the approval already obtained to remain in the meeting record. He agreed. Lena acknowledged her company's action items. The later stage's start date remained blank.

## The Last Page

After the budget meeting, the client authorized Lena to review the formal scope documents in the project room. She opened only the sections covering this expansion and the existing services directly affected by the decision.

In the client's written decision, she selected “Scope of This Round” and enlarged the entries:

```text
New deployment for this round: not activated
Dedicated follow-on engineering / review resources: no longer reserved
Existing service contracts: continue
Completed preparation work: settle under the agreed terms
```

ACL's authorized representative withdrew the expansion proposal and the implementation-example submission. Lena opened the secretariat's email. Only that example's status had changed in the linked agenda. Discussion of methodology 7.3 remained.

She checked the project name and VeriTest's completed work. Her seat and the acknowledgment of her recusal were both still in the membership portal.

The settlement page listed the preparation work and limited review VeriTest had completed. Lena crossed the planned follow-on reports and ongoing service revenue out of the company plan, leaving the costs already incurred. Leo's separate confirmation removed the later rollout engineering. The integration and records would still be delivered as agreed.

The appendix retained the parent's VeriTest contract and ACL's services for the overseas subsidiary and internal business line. Lena checked off the retained scope the client had authorized her to review.

She filed the working draft separately from the checked run records. The material-use and retention terms had not changed.

**This round's expansion had been withdrawn. What had already been learned had not.**

She turned to the last page, checked the signing parties, then returned to the project scope on page one. The withdrawn project name matched the original notice.

**The documents said nothing about the state of ACL's business elsewhere.**

Outside, the night had deepened. Thunder rolled over the roof. Rain drummed against the glass.
