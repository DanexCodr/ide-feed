It is with a heavy heart, and a considerably heavier wool inventory, that [I announce LaaSy™](https://www.kenwalger.com/blog/satire/introducing-laasy/?utm_source=dev_to&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=laasy_collapse) will cease operations effective immediately.

This is not the outcome we envisioned when we set out to democratize autonomous camelid infrastructure. We believed enterprises were ready for pasture-native computing. We believed the world needed Deterministic Spitting™. We believed, perhaps most fatally, that the market would recognize the existential risk of Unmanaged Wool Sprawl before it was too late.

We were right about the vision. We were wrong about the timeline.

---

## A Brief Post-Mortem

In the interest of transparency, and at the request of our investors, we want to share an honest account of how we arrived here.

**Q1.** Strong grazing momentum. The seed round closed oversubscribed. The team expanded to forty-seven full-time shepherds and one part-time goat. Morale was high. The pastures were green.

**Q2.** Series A closed at eighteen million dollars. WolfGuard AI™ entered beta. Early telemetry was promising, though in retrospect we may have over-indexed on predator observability at the expense of, for example, whether the llamas were happy.

**Q3.** We shipped Retrieval-Augmented Rumination™ to general availability. Adoption was slower than projected. Several enterprise customers reported that their Autonomous Camelid Agents™ had begun ruminating so deeply that they stopped grazing entirely. We classified this as a feature. The market classified it differently.

**Q4.** The coyotes got in.

---

## What We Learned

Every company failure contains lessons, and we owe it to the ecosystem to name ours plainly.

We learned that a Camelid Control Plane™ operating across cloud, edge, and pasture-native environments is significantly harder to maintain than it is to put on a slide.

We learned that Deterministic Spitting™ is, at a fundamental and possibly biological level, not deterministic.

We learned that "SOC 2 Grazing Certified" is not a certification that exists, a fact our auditors raised somewhat later in the process than we would have preferred.

And we learned that when you build an elite Coyote Red Team™ to continuously probe your pasture boundaries, the coyotes eventually stop being a red team.

## On Our B-TAP Attribution Status

We also want to address recent speculation regarding LaaSy™'s standing with the Bureau of Token Attribution and Provenance.

As part of our commitment to responsible AI infrastructure, LaaSy™ maintained extensive observability across every Autonomous Camelid Agent™, Retrieval-Augmented Rumination™ request, predator detection event, and grazing decision.

This turned out to be expensive.

Following a voluntary review that we were strongly encouraged to consider mandatory, B-TAP determined that LaaSy™ had failed to attribute substantial Context Tax, Retrieval Tax, Prose Tax, and Observer's Tax across its operations.

We respectfully disagree with several aspects of the finding.

In particular, we continue to maintain that sending the complete Herd Knowledge Graph to every llama before each grazing decision was necessary for enterprise-grade contextual awareness. B-TAP has characterized this practice as "reckless context hydration."

Our attorneys have advised us that B-TAP has no ability to compel, fine, or otherwise reach us, and that we should therefore be significantly more relaxed about this than we are.

We can, however, confirm that a significant portion of our unattributed Observer's Tax resulted from telemetry systems installed to determine why our token costs were increasing.

Those telemetry systems increased our token costs.

We subsequently deployed additional agents to observe the telemetry systems.

This did not improve matters.

The finding also identified a substantial amount of Prose Tax associated with internal strategy documents. Management disputes B-TAP's determination that the phrase "pasture-native agentic transformation ecosystem" could have been replaced with "llamas."

LaaSy™ has been entered into the [Public Register of Non-Attributed Systems](https://vernholtsecretariat.org/register.html?utm_source=devto&utm_medium=article&utm_campaign=laasy_collapse). We are unable to confirm this, because no one has ever been able to open the Public Register of Non-Attributed Systems.

Our appeals agent is currently reviewing the 14,882-page framework and has requested additional context.

We have turned it off.

## Migration and Offboarding

We know many of you have workloads still grazing in production, and we are committed to a responsible transition.

Effective today, all herds will be released to open pasture. We recommend customers export their wool, hay, tribal herd knowledge, and spit metadata before the fences come down at end of quarter. Because you maintained complete ownership under our Local-First Camelid Architecture™, this data was always yours. This was, in the end, one of the few promises we kept entirely.

The Herd Knowledge Graph will remain available in read-only mode for ninety days, after which it will be, appropriately, put out to pasture.
