We all know the classic icebreaker: three statements, two true, one a lie. Today we're playing it with a developer twist.

Below are three rounds. Pick the lie in each one, then check the answers at the bottom. No Googling, and no cheating!

## Round 1: Origins

1. The first computer "bug" was a real moth found stuck in a relay of the Harvard Mark II in 1947.
2. Python is named after the snake because its creator loved reptiles.
3. The first working version of JavaScript was famously built in about 10 days.

## Round 2: Tech history

1. One of the first 1 GB hard drives, IBM's 3380 from 1980, weighed over 500 pounds.
2. Git was created by Linus Torvalds after a licensing dispute over the tool the Linux kernel team was using.
3. The very first website ever published is no longer online.

## Round 3: Your turn (about me)

1. [Something true about you, e.g. "I once deleted a production table"]
2. [Something true about you, e.g. "My first language was PHP"]
3. [Something false but believable]

Which one is the lie? Drop your guess in the comments!

## Answers

**Round 1:** Statement 2 is the lie. Python is named after the British comedy group *Monty Python*, not the snake. The moth story is true, and the JavaScript story is widely told.

**Round 2:** Statement 3 is the lie. CERN restored the world's first website, and you can still visit it today.

**Round 3:** I'll reveal mine in the comments once a few of you have guessed!
