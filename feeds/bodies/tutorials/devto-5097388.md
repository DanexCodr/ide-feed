*An Ancient Idea in a Digital World*

---

It's 1:30 a.m.

The café is still open, although only a few lights above the counter are on.

I'm wiping down the coffee machine when the little bell above the door rings.

A young man walks in with a laptop bag over his shoulder. His eyes look tired, and he looks like he's had a very long day.

"Are you still open?"

"You're in luck. You look like you need a coffee more than I do."

He laughs.

"I've just finished a deployment. Things are stable for now. I need a coffee. Something strong."

"For now?"

"Anyone working in IT knows what that means. You never know what's coming next."

I make him an espresso.

"Sounds like you've had a day full of changes."

"Don't get me started. Requirements change, technologies change, teams change. Sometimes I just wish the whole world would pause for a moment."

I slide the coffee across the counter.

"There's an old book on my shelf that might have something to say about that."

He follows my finger and looks at the bookshelf.

"The I Ching?"

"That's the one."

"I've heard of it. Isn't it some kind of fortune-telling book?"

"That's what a lot of people associate it with. But there's something else about it that I find more interesting."

"What's that?"

"Change."

He takes a sip.

"People were studying change thousands of years ago?"

"More seriously than you might think."

---

## An Old Book, Two Lines

I take the old book from the shelf and grab a paper napkin.

On it, I draw one solid horizontal line, then another line with a gap in the middle.

"These are called Yang and Yin lines in the I Ching."

He looks at them.

"Two lines?"

"That's right. For the sake of our little conversation, you can think of the Yang line as 1 and the Yin line as 0."


![Image1](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/sh1nggpp2q4i4i4yrasf.jpg)

His expression changes.

"Hold on. Zero and one?"

"What about them?"

"Isn't that just binary? The foundation of computing?"

I smile.

"Programmers. You see two lines and immediately think of binary."

"Well, can you blame me? What else can you do with them?"

"Add another line."

---

## Eight Trigrams on a Napkin

I draw a few combinations of three lines on another napkin.

"Three lines can be combined in eight different ways. These are the Eight Trigrams."

I write a few numbers beside the drawings:

000, 001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110, 111.

We read each trigram from the bottom line upward.


![Image2](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/odrm192jr7qw67e6apmo.jpg)

He stares at the numbers, picks up his phone, and taps a few buttons.

"Two to the power of three. Eight!"

"Exactly."

"Tell me something. What about six lines?"

"Sixty-four."

I write on the edge of the napkin:

**2⁶ = 64**


![Image3](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/cymbrlkkjnm9gy5nf9wk.jpg)

He studies it for a moment, then laughs.

"You're turning the I Ching into a binary tutorial."

"I'm just using a language you're more familiar with."

---

## The Interesting Part Is the Change

He takes another sip of coffee.

"So the sixty-four hexagrams are just sixty-four fixed patterns?"

I shake my head.

"That would be missing the interesting part."

"Why?"

I pick up the pen and change one of the lines in a hexagram.

**"Because the lines can change. A Yin line can become Yang, and a Yang line can become Yin. When a line changes, the whole hexagram may become something else."**

He watches the little drawing.

"So the point isn't just to remember what it is, but to see what it might become?"

"More or less. You also have to look at why it's changing."

He leans back in his chair. For the first time tonight, he seems genuinely interested in something other than his deployment.

"That's a bit like working with systems. Something can look fine when it goes live, but that doesn't mean it'll stay that way. More users, more data, changing business needs... suddenly the architecture that worked yesterday doesn't quite fit anymore."

"Mmm."

"Teams are the same. Development, testing, product, infrastructure. They all affect one another. A change in one place means the others have to adjust, too."

I look at his cup.

"You haven't even finished your coffee, and you've already started talking about work again."

He laughs.

"Occupational hazard. Can't help it."

---

## It Also Depends on Where You Stand

I take away his empty espresso cup and set a fresh glass of water beside him.

"There's another interesting detail in the I Ching."

"Go on."

"Besides looking at whether a line is Yin or Yang, you also look at where it sits."

"Where it sits?"

**"The same Yang line can mean something different depending on its position. The same goes for a Yin line."**

"Some positions are considered suitable for Yang, others for Yin."

He thinks for a moment.

"So it's not just about whether someone has the ability to do something. It's also about whether they're in the right position?"

"Not a bad way to put it."

"Sounds a bit like organizational management again."

"Looks like tonight's coffee wasn't wasted."

In a six-line hexagram, the positions are counted from the bottom up. The first, third, and fifth positions are Yang positions. The second, fourth, and sixth are Yin positions. A Yang line in a Yang position, or a Yin line in a Yin position, is generally considered to be in its proper place.

![Image4](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/xzfs597r0ajpri64e7ee.jpg)

He picks up his water glass, only to discover that it's empty again.

"So, what exactly is dynamic balance?"

---

## Balance Doesn't Mean Standing Still

I'm about to answer when the coffee machine makes a little warning sound.

I turn to deal with it. When I come back, he's still staring at the napkin.

"Well, **balance doesn't necessarily mean both sides are equal. And it certainly doesn't mean everything has to stand still.**"

"Then what does it mean?"

"Most of the sixty-four hexagrams describe a kind of balance that's still moving. A few are special cases, but the interesting thing is how the situation keeps changing."

"Sometimes one side grows while the other recedes. Sometimes a situation changes, the old balance is lost, and a new one gradually takes shape."

He nods.

"So balance itself keeps changing."

"Now you're getting somewhere."


![Image5](https://dev-to-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/articles/jjt6s8zt12p6pbd8wfmw.jpg)

He suddenly laughs.

"You know, I only came here tonight for a coffee. I didn't expect to end up studying ancient Chinese philosophy."

"That happens."

"Really?"

"Late-night cafés have a way of bringing out conversations that might never happen during the day."

He glances at his laptop, then back at the napkin.

"Next time, you'll have to tell me about all sixty-four hexagrams."

I collect his empty glass.

"Sure."

"Really?"

"Of course."

"Great!"

"But that's enough for tonight."

"Why?"

"You've had enough philosophy for one night, and I need a break, too."

He laughs.

"So there's a cover charge for the philosophy?"

"Naturally. Did you think I could explain several thousand years of ideas over a single cup?"

We both laugh.

He folds the napkin and slips it into his laptop bag.

Just before leaving, he turns around and asks one more question.

"So, do you think change is good or bad?"

I pick up the cloth and continue wiping the counter.

"That's a question for your next visit."

He pushes the door open. The little bell rings.

It's 2:40 a.m.

I look at the I Ching on the counter, then at the computer in the corner of the café, its screen still glowing softly.

**Sometimes we're so busy looking for a fixed answer that we forget to notice what's already changing right in front of us.**

**Systems change. Environments change. People change.**

And the lights in this café aren't going out just yet.

After all, someone still hasn't had their next cup of coffee.
