Let me ask you something. What are you supposed to learn when the CEOs of all AI companies keep claiming that programming will be dead in a few years?

If you were starting college this year, wouldn't you be worried about spending years mastering a skill that might become obsolete by the time you graduate?

I was.

Actually, I was worried even before AI models got this good.

I was afraid to choose game development, even though I liked it, because there seemed to be fewer opportunities than in app development. I was afraid to choose app development because there seemed to be fewer jobs than in web development. And I was afraid to choose web development because **ChatGPT told me it was one of the areas of software engineering most vulnerable to AI automation.**

I'm ashamed to admit. But that's how I ended up looking into embedded systems. I kept jumping from one field to another, hoping to find the *right* one before it was too late.

But how do you chase a moving target when every step toward it feels like a step away from everything you've built?

That's one thing. But imagine knowing (or thinking, to be precise) that programming itself, as we know it, could become obsolete within just a few years. I dont know much about AI, so I cannot reliabily decide whether everyone else who *knows* about AI is right or wrong.

However, I cannot sit there and wait for the future to arrive so I can start learning. I had to make my bet. But how?

I wanted some help. So I posted this on DEV.to:

> *Graduating in 2030: Is learning C worth it? Honestly?*

So I did.

I stopped **right where I was standing** - embedded systems development using ESP32 microcontrollers and the native ESP-IDF framework. I was right on the verge of leaving ESP32 and going for STM32 because I thought it was more industry-relevant in my area. But that plan was temporarily cancelled (though I might revisit STM32 in the future as a side skill) because he asked me to specialize rather than be the person who uses what's "demanding".

But about the "start building projects" part, that was something I had trouble with. I had several project ideas related to general software engineering (and I still have so many just sitting around), but never embedded systems engineering. So what did I build?

I built a fully free embedded systems course focusing on the ESP-IDF framework, teaching the basics of embedded systems development. The course has a very specific goal: to learn to work with AI.

Even though @francistrdev was the first to comment, several other people, like @fm @unitbuilds @build996 @aniruddhaadak @elanatframework and a few others gave me extremely valuable advices. My course is based on the *average* opinion of all the comments. I saw 2 opposite viewpoints and everything in between, so I couldn't do anything but go with a middle ground.

And remember that I had trouble coming up with projects related to embedded systems? But that's no longer the case! I don't know if it's because I built that course, but I started getting ideas once I finished the "Basics" section! 🎉 I will be posting here once I complete it. I am waiting for the hardware delivery! I am sure others will like it 🙂
