> The Thirty-Six Stratagems, [Stratagem 36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-Six_Stratagems)

## Open as Usual

When Mark pushed the door, it opened only halfway. A roll of protective paper lay against it, its loose end scuffed by passing shoes. He stepped over it, turned, and pressed the curl flat with his foot. The door swung back on its own. The chime rang twice.

The shop smelled of freshly opened cardboard boxes. The small table where Mark usually sat had been moved, leaving four pale squares on the floor by the wall. At the other end, chairs stood stacked upside down by the window, a sheet of measurements tucked between their legs.

A sign on the door read, “Open during renovations.”

The heat was on. So was the coffee machine. **The man** stood behind the counter, putting a freshly dried cup back on the shelf. He picked up another and worked the cloth slowly around its rim.

Mark sent Lena a message:

“Still open.”

Last time they had arranged to meet for coffee, Lena had arrived first and sent him a picture of an empty chair taken through the window. When he put his phone down, the typing indicator appeared for a few seconds, then disappeared.

The large table in the middle of the shop was still empty. **The man** set a pour-over near the edge. Mark nudged his laptop bag under the table with his shoe and sat on the side of the table nearest the wall.

The coffee was still hot.

He set the cup down and opened his laptop. A few files from last time were still in the local folder. He checked their last-modified dates without opening them, then minimized the window.

**The man** was already back behind the counter, working the cloth around the bottom of a cup to wipe away the last of the water.

## What She Said in the Parking Lot

The chime at the door rang twice.

Derek came in, glanced at the new layout, nodded to Mark, and walked over to the large table. He took off his coat, draped it over the next chair, and sat down.

**The man** made him an Americano and set it on the table. Derek took a sip, his eyes resting briefly on the upturned chairs.

“Quite a change,” he said.

“They're not done yet,” Mark said.

Derek smiled and said nothing more.

The chime rang twice. Lena came in, saw Derek, and nodded as she walked over.

Derek moved his coat from the chair beside him to an empty one behind him. She tucked her scarf into her bag and sat down.

“You left something out,” she told Mark.

“What?”

“You can't get to the window seats. You only said it was open. I thought I could sit in my usual spot.”

**The man** put a long black beside her. Lena took a couple of sips before setting her bag at her feet.

Derek looked at her, then asked:

“Remember what you said in the parking lot?”

Lena looked up.

“Which part?”

“To make sure my boss knew what I was doing before I went any further.”

Lena paused, then smiled.

“I remember. I wasn't very polite about it.”

“No, you weren't.”

Derek lifted his Americano, then set it down.

“Back then, all I could think about was how I'd lost. I didn't take in a word you said.”

“And now?”

“Now I get it. **You weren't telling me how to win.**”

Lena said nothing.

Derek looked down at the table for a moment.

“You were telling me not to commit the company to something it hadn't agreed to. Just because I put the proposal together didn't mean my boss had signed off.”

Lena took a sip of her long black.

“You've got it this time.”

“Took me long enough.”

“Better than never getting it.”

Derek smiled.

Mark stayed out of it. He let them talk.

## Old Records

The chime rang twice. P came in. Mark moved his laptop bag farther under the table, and Lena tucked hers beneath her chair. P took an empty seat.

Mark and P exchanged a nod. **The man** set an espresso beside P.

P took a sip.

Mark brought the window back up on his laptop. The old copy P had supplied was still in the local folder, beside the calculation and output he had saved last time. The twenty-two values were actual arrival times, not scheduled send times. The file's last-modified date was still visible.

He did not run the script again. He only viewed the saved code excerpt and the old output.

```python
deltas = [b - a for a, b in zip(times, times[1:])]
print("checks:", len(times), "intervals:", len(deltas))
print("unique_seconds:", sorted(set(deltas)))
```

```text
checks: 22 intervals: 21
unique_seconds: [Decimal('900.000')]
```

Mark put the old output beside his index and rewrote the entry:

**Intervals checked. Source unverified.**

He saved the index. The old output stayed unchanged.

He turned the screen back toward himself. P did not lean in.

Only when Mark closed the laptop did P ask, “Still haven't found the source?”

“No.”

“Leave it there for now, then.”

“Yeah.”

P glanced at the laptop on the table.

“Don't fill in what you don't know just to make it look complete.”

Mark pushed the laptop closer to his bag.

“I wasn't going to.”

P asked nothing more.

**The man** picked up an empty cup at the counter and worked the cloth around its rim.

## Still on the Agenda

The chime at the door rang twice.

Leo came in, looked toward Lena, and held up his phone.

“You said there was somewhere to sit. Didn't mention there was only one option.”

“You can stand,” Lena said, pulling the next chair out a little. “It'll look less crowded.”

Leo sat down and put his phone facedown on the table.

Lena's screen showed the council's public agenda. Two markets were listed at the top: the EU and Singapore. Farther down, “Cross-Sector AI Validation Methodology” ran over two lines.

“Still looking at that?” Leo asked. “I don't even need to keep the extra staff on standby anymore.”

“The existing services are still running,” Lena said, her finger stopping beneath the heading.

“What about the project?”

“The client isn't reserving any more staff time for the follow-on work. ACL withdrew the implementation example it had submitted.”

“Are you handling that whole client group now?”

“Just the parent company's annual certification,” Lena said. “ACL still handles the overseas subsidiary and another business line within the parent company.”

Leo glanced at the screen.

“They're still discussing how to do the validation?”

“Still on the agenda.”

“Doesn't sound finished.”

“Never said it was.”

Mark lifted his pour-over. The cup stopped at his lips, then came back down.

“I asked Caleb the other day what happens to the validation procedures when a project ends.”

Lena turned toward him.

“What did he say?”

“He said he'd organized validation methods from healthcare and finance projects. Different clients, but the approaches from both ended up in the same validation handbook.”

Leo took his hand off his phone. “He works on both?”

“He says he just organizes the methods. He doesn't do the projects.”

Lena looked at the agenda on her phone.

“Anything to do with this one?”

“He didn't say. I haven't seen the whole handbook either.”

Lena nodded and left it there.

**The man** brought the flat white. Leo pulled his phone back to make room. Lena closed the agenda, turned off her screen, and took a sip of her long black.

Mark held his cup without bringing the laptop out again.

A few seconds later, Leo turned his phone over and answered a message.

## Putting a Face to the Message

The chime rang twice. Alex came in with a softcover notebook under his arm, nodded to Derek, and raised a hand to Mark.

**The man** set a latte beside him.

Alex opened the notebook, found a time listed for tomorrow, crossed out the appointment, and moved it to the next page. Mark glanced over without asking what he had changed.

Alex left his pen in the gutter.

Derek glanced at the page. “Put enough detail in this time?”

“It's easier to leave things out when it's just for me. Give it a few days and I'll be guessing too,” Alex said.

“Same with code?”

“I did once, when I read someone else's code. Not a single comment. Didn't even know their name. I left them a message complimenting the comments.”

Mark laughed.

“That's a pretty low bar for a compliment.”

“I did want to say something nice.”

P set the espresso back on its saucer and leaned slightly toward the table.

“Found you.”

Alex did not reach for his latte.

The pen rolled a little along the gutter and stopped against his wrist. He looked up at P.

“That message was to you?”

**“You kept the compliment short. I kept the reply short.”**

P looked at him.

“Channel confirmed.”

Alex looked at P.

“So it was you.”

P turned the spoon handle inward over the saucer. Alex did not take out his phone. P did not reach into a pocket.

Neither said anything more.

Derek glanced at them without asking. Leo was answering messages; Lena was looking at her phone. Mark set down his pour-over with a soft click.

Alex bent over the notebook again and finished writing down the time he had moved to the next page. Neither of them explained the exchange to anyone else.

## One by One

Alex finished his latte first.

**The man** came over with two dry saucers stacked in his hands. Alex set the empty cup near the edge and waited until **the man** had a hand free before passing it over. He tucked the notebook under his arm and put the pen in his coat pocket.

“I'm heading out.”

Derek nodded.

Alex pushed the door open and left. The chime rang twice.

Leo finished the last of his flat white, answered the messages on his phone, and stood to put on his coat. He gave the others a small wave. No one got up. Then he went out. The chime rang twice.

P finished the espresso and set the cup back on its saucer.

Mark started to move his chair. P touched the backrest, said there was no need, and slipped past. At the door, P stopped and looked back at Lena.

“That guy who just left. Pretty good-looking.”

Lena looked up.

“Leo?”

“You knew who I meant.”

P smiled and went out. The chime rang twice.

Lena said nothing. She finished a few replies on her phone, then drank the rest of her long black.

Derek was still looking at his calendar. Lena picked up her bag, nodded to both of them, and left. The chime rang twice.

Derek finished his Americano before getting up. He took his coat from the chair behind him, put it on, and went out. The chime rang twice.

## The Laptop Behind the Bar

Only Mark remained at the large table.

**The man** took away the empty Americano cup and wiped the tabletop. Near Mark, he folded the cloth to a clean side and worked around the laptop.

Mark lifted his pour-over out of the way and took the last sip. The coffee was no longer hot. He tipped the cup back and drank what remained.

He put the closed laptop in his bag. The zipper caught halfway; he pushed the laptop farther in and tried again.

Mark slid his chair under the table and carried the empty cup to the counter.

**The man** was putting cups into the sink. Mark set his on a dry patch of counter, the handle facing out.

**The man** finished with the other cups. Water struck their bases with a hollow sound. When the tap stopped, **the man** dried his hands and looked up. The pour-over cup was still on the counter.

The old laptop behind the bar was closed, its edges worn to a paler shade. The power cord ran down beside it and under the counter.

Mark stayed, his hand still resting on the cup handle. He looked at the laptop for a moment, then back at **the man**.

The heat was still on. The curled paper at the entrance was caught beneath the door, and the lights behind the bar were reflected in the glass. The large table was empty, a few chairs still out of place.

Mark glanced at the closed laptop.

“Every time I come in, that thing's closed.”

He let go of the cup handle.

“What do you write on it when nobody's here?”

**The man** smiled slightly and said nothing.

Mark hitched the bag strap higher on his shoulder, walked between the table and the door, stepped over the curled paper, and pushed the door open. He was the last to leave the Third Cup. The chime rang twice.

This is the stratagem: [Know When to Walk Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-Six_Stratagems). **What had been established stayed on record. What remained unknown had not been turned into an answer.**

They had finished their coffee without going over old ground again.

---

**The man** watched the door until it stopped swinging.

The last pour-over cup was still on the counter.

He washed it and dried it. The cloth moved slowly around the rim, then across the base. A streak of water remained on the side. He turned the cup and wiped it again.

He held it up to the light, checked that no water marks remained, and put it back on the shelf.

**The thirty-sixth.**

**The man** washed his hands, sat down behind the bar, and opened the laptop.
